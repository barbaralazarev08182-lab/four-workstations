# VQ2 Chapter 1 Gameplay Retention Redesign — 2026-09-04

> Status: USER FEEDBACK LOCKED / GAMEPLAY REDESIGN PROPOSAL OPEN
>
> User feedback: the current Chapter 1 narrative is strong and works very well as story, but as a playable first chapter it is not yet dense or compelling enough to maximize first-hour retention. The next design pass must increase meaningful player agency, tactile interaction, reward cadence, and use of the core Retrieval/room systems without turning VQ2 into a busy action game.

---

## 1. Locked product lesson

The opening cannot rely mainly on:

- dialogue;
- atmosphere;
- passive observation;
- guided furniture setup;
- one large spectacle near the end.

Those remain valuable, but the first hour must repeatedly give the player something they personally **do**, **change**, **discover**, or **choose**.

Target emotional commitments by the end of Chapter 1:

1. **I want to live here.** — ownership of the room.
2. **I want to retrieve one more thing.** — attachment to the core gameplay loop.
3. **I want to know what is outside.** — narrative mystery.

The game should not attempt to retain literally every possible player. The practical goal is to make the first hour demonstrate VQ2's unique value early and repeatedly.

---

## 2. Current weakness diagnosis

The previous story-first Chapter 1 draft has several gameplay risks:

- too much of the first half can be spent following the guide drone;
- the player has limited meaningful choice early;
- Retrieval is explained before it becomes a memorable hands-on mechanic;
- room furnishing risks reading as tutorial chores unless it produces obvious ownership/reward;
- the first-hour battle is visually impressive but primarily passive;
- the first chapter lacks a strong interactive payoff immediately after the spectacle;
- the player should finish the chapter wanting to continue playing, not merely wanting to know what happens next.

---

## 3. Proposed first-hour retention spine

This is a proposal, not yet a locked implementation contract.

### 0–5 min — Arrival / Immediate Agency

Goals:

- player gains movement/camera control almost immediately;
- the open two-wall room and impossible exterior are visible very early;
- the drone gives only essential instruction;
- player can inspect several objects in any order rather than follow a rigid single-file tutorial.

Recommended first meaningful choice before minute 5:

- choose which starter object / utility to activate or place first;
- choice does not create a punitive permanent branch, but changes the immediate room state.

Retention hook:

> “This place is impossible, but it is already responding to what I do.”

### 5–15 min — Claim the Room

Player opens the supply crate and performs one short sequence that visibly changes the room.

Possible actions:

- activate the local wireless Power Hub;
- place one or two useful domestic objects;
- choose where a small object belongs using the accepted placement/grid logic;
- turn on a display, lamp, charging surface, storage module, or similar domestic system.

Important rule:

The player should not assemble five pieces of furniture as chores. One or two interactions should create a strong visual before/after difference.

Reward:

- an area of the room comes alive;
- light / power / display behavior changes;
- the player is responsible for the improvement.

Retention hook:

> “I am not merely trapped here; I can shape this place.”

### 15–25 min — First Atlas / Boundary Contradiction

Introduce a physical mystery the player can investigate rather than only hear about.

Possible sequence:

- artificial sun / light direction changes;
- a small Atlas-related visual disturbance appears;
- a thrown/test object or sensor demonstrates that the visible floor edge is not the true refuge boundary;
- the drone prevents a reckless test but provides enough evidence for the player to infer a larger invisible stable volume.

Do not explain the entire refuge cosmology.

Player should perform at least one observation or test that produces a result.

Retention hook:

> “The room is not what it looks like.”

### 25–38 min — First Real Retrieval

Retrieval should become playable in Chapter 1, not merely described.

Recommended first Retrieval:

- short range;
- low danger;
- clearly visible target;
- real powered tether behavior;
- Orb visibly crosses the Atlas boundary;
- the boundary briefly becomes perceptible through distortion / ripple / light;
- room power or comfort lighting visibly dips when the breach opens;
- player feels the cost physically, not through a tooltip.

The first target should be understandable enough to create satisfaction but strange enough to produce curiosity.

Do not start with a giant lore artifact.

The first Retrieval should teach the full emotional loop:

`choose target -> deploy Orb -> cross boundary -> establish connection -> pull -> room reacts -> object arrives physically on the floor`

Retention hook:

> “I can physically bring pieces of the Outside into my home.”

### 38–48 min — Use / Preserve / Reinterpret

The retrieved object must immediately prove that Retrieval is not just collection.

The player should be able to do at least two meaningful things with it, for example:

- reclaim part/all of it for material or energy;
- preserve it for inspection;
- place it somewhere;
- connect it to Atlas or another system;
- use it to restore a small room function.

Avoid an early punishing irreversible choice. The first example should teach the principle without making inexperienced players feel they chose “wrong.”

Recommended approach:

- retrieved object contains ordinary recoverable material plus one anomalous component that cannot be reduced normally;
- player receives both immediate practical value and a mystery.

Reward:

- a visible room improvement is funded by the player's own Retrieval;
- the anomalous remnant creates a small later reaction.

Retention hook:

> “Everything I bring back can change the room in more than one way.”

### 48–60 min — Distant War Becomes Interactive

Keep the locked distant-war spectacle, but do not make the player a passive audience for the entire climax.

Proposed sequence:

1. player is already using / preparing the Retrieval system or interacting with the room;
2. distant fleet arrives and battle begins;
3. the player can freely move, watch, hide behind structure, approach the open edge, or keep working;
4. the annihilating strike destroys the fleet;
5. delayed disturbance reaches the refuge;
6. lights / Power Hub / Retrieval systems dip or temporarily destabilize;
7. the player performs a short, previously learned recovery action rather than a cinematic QTE;
8. after stabilization, a small piece of distant battle debris / anomalous signal appears in or near future Retrieval range.

The player must remain a small observer, not a combat participant.

The war's identities remain unknown.

Retention hook:

> “That impossible event outside just changed something inside my room.”

---

## 4. Recommended Chapter 1 ending

The chapter should not end immediately after the war.

It should end with an **interactive cliffhanger**.

Strong candidate:

- the delayed shock reaches Atlas;
- the refuge stabilizes;
- one new contact / signal / debris signature appears outside;
- the object is unlike the first tutorial Retrieval target;
- Atlas reacts in a way not previously seen;
- the drone cannot fully explain it;
- the game gives control back to the player.

The desired final thought is not only:

> “What was that battle?”

but also:

> **“Can I bring that thing back?”**

This converts narrative curiosity into gameplay desire.

---

## 5. Interaction-density target

Proposed first-hour cadence:

- meaningful control in first 60–90 seconds;
- first player-authored room change by ~10 min;
- first physical mystery interaction by ~20 min;
- first real Retrieval by ~30 min;
- first systemic cost/reward consequence by ~40 min;
- major spectacle around ~50 min;
- interactive aftermath / next Retrieval hook by ~60 min.

Avoid long periods where the player only walks behind the drone and listens.

---

## 6. What must NOT be added merely for retention

Do not solve retention by adding generic systems that contradict VQ2:

- no combat tutorial in Chapter 1;
- no loot rarity shower;
- no quest-log spam;
- no five-currency economy tutorial;
- no crafting-tree dump;
- no forced QTE spectacle;
- no long cinematic that takes control away;
- no constant objective arrows;
- no busywork furniture assembly;
- no fake “urgent” timers unless fiction and gameplay genuinely require them.

Retention should come from agency, physicality, discovery, ownership, and consequence.

---

## 7. Core Chapter 1 gameplay promise

By the end of the first hour, the player should have personally experienced a small version of the full game:

`Inhabit -> Retrieve -> Inspect -> Place/Use -> Observe -> Reinterpret`

The first chapter is therefore not only exposition. It is a compact playable proof of the entire VQ2 product fantasy.
