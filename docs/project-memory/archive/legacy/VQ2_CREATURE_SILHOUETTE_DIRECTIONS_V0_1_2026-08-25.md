# VQ2 Creature Silhouette Directions v0.1

> Date: 2026-08-25  
> Status: CONCEPT STUDY / PROPOSED — NO FINAL CREATURE SELECTED  
> Scope: inhabitant silhouette exploration, animation cost, world-fit, interaction anatomy, four-player readability  
> Parent direction: `VQ2_INHABITANT_CREATURE_DIRECTION_V0_1_2026-08-25.md`

---

# 0. Goal

The project has already selected a **body-plan direction** as the strongest proposal:

> clearly non-human visual identity + one unified soft body/head mass + two short arms + two short legs + simple hand/foot pads + a small Generic/custom rig.

This document does **not** pick a final species.

Its purpose is to create several silhouettes that all satisfy the same gameplay/production contract while feeling materially different from one another.

The final choice must be judged in the real Office camera, not from a close-up concept sheet alone.

---

# 1. Non-negotiable silhouette rules

Regardless of which concept wins:

- body/head should read primarily as one mass rather than a miniature human torso plus head;
- no thin human neck;
- no realistic shoulders/clavicles;
- no individual finger requirement;
- no long fragile limbs;
- no long tail unless a future gameplay verb specifically justifies it;
- two arms must remain capable of reaching a workbench, item, Atlas control, and salvage grip;
- two legs should support walking/waddling and dangling visibly from an open edge;
- the lower body must support an explicit `BodySeatContact` rather than human pelvis alignment;
- one strong silhouette feature may differentiate identity, but it should not require a different skeleton topology;
- the design must read under the fixed miniature/isometric camera;
- four residents should still look related, as if they belong to the same world/body family.

Permanent visual test:

> If the character is filled completely black, its basic body family and at least some player variants should still be recognizable.

Color is identity support, not the only identity channel.

# 2. Direction A — Seed / Bud Resident

Working shorthand: `SEED`

## Shape

- compact teardrop / seed / pear body;
- slightly narrower lower body;
- two very short legs;
- short side arms;
- one non-literal top growth feature: folded bud, blunt sprout, soft stalk, or paired leaf-like lobes;
- body can subtly elongate when reaching and compress when resting.

Conceptual black silhouette:

```text
       /\
      /  \
     (    )
   --(    )--
      \  /
      |  |
```

The top feature should **not** become a direct Pikmin-style flower-on-stalk copy.

## World fit

Strong.

It naturally fits a world containing:

- cultivation;
- recovered seeds/spores;
- staged growth;
- soft organic anomalies;
- a room that may itself behave partly like a living system.

However, the inhabitants' origin should not automatically be explained as plant biology.

## Animation fit

Very strong.

- body tilt can replace realistic spine acting;
- arms can stretch slightly when needed;
- legs can dangle cleanly;
- sleep can become a closed/curled bud-like pose;
- surprise can stiffen/open the top feature.

## Interaction fit

Very strong.

- clear hand sockets;
- clear body-bottom seat contact;
- simple pulling silhouette;
- good desk/workbench access.

## Risks

- too literal a sprout makes it derivative of Pikmin;
- too botanical can make later mechanical/Atlas work visually contradictory;
- if every player differs only by flower/leaf, identity can feel costume-like rather than bodily.

## Assessment

> Strong candidate, especially if kept ambiguous between seed, bud, soft organism, and unknown resident rather than explicitly "plant creature."

# 3. Direction B — Soft Pebble / Clay Resident

Working shorthand: `PEBBLE`

## Shape

- rounded irregular pebble / kneaded-clay body;
- slightly asymmetrical top line;
- low center of mass;
- arms emerge directly from body sides;
- short broad feet;
- identity markers appear as dents, folds, small soft fins, nubs, ridges, or attached tags rather than hair.

Conceptual black silhouette:

```text
      ______
    /        \
   /          \
  (            )
 --(            )--
    \________/
      |    |
```

The body should feel deformable even if its resting shape suggests a stone or ceramic lump.

## World fit

Very strong.

This direction fits the project's material language unusually well:

- ceramic;
- soft matte clay;
- handmade model;
- worn / repaired surfaces;
- recovered fragments;
- architecture and construction.

It also avoids assigning the inhabitants an obvious Earth-animal or plant category.

## Animation fit

Excellent.

- squat body makes balance easy;
- body squash/stretch looks natural;
- sitting can be a literal soft compression onto the floor edge;
- pulling can lean the whole mass backward without human joint realism;
- resting can flatten/curl into a compact lump.

## Interaction fit

Excellent.

This is probably the easiest of all candidates for:

- edge sitting;
- carrying;
- salvage pulling;
- Atlas repair;
- heavy construction gestures.

## Risks

- can become visually too static or expressionless;
- can read as "cute rock" if surface treatment is too literal;
- needs very strong eyes/top feature/pose timing to avoid feeling like a prop.

## Assessment

> Highest current production-confidence candidate. Strongest if we want the residents to feel impossible to categorize biologically.

# 4. Direction C — Soft Toy / Pouch Resident

Working shorthand: `POUCH`

## Shape

- rounded fabric-pouch / dumpling / cushion volume;
- soft lower corners;
- floppy short arms;
- tiny legs or feet emerging below;
- optional seam/fold-like top silhouette;
- can visibly wrinkle/compress during actions.

Conceptual black silhouette:

```text
      __/\__
    /        \
   /          \
  |            |
 --|            |--
   \__________/
      |      |
```

The final material does **not** have to be literal cloth. "Pouch" describes volume/behavior, not canon biology.

## World fit

Strong aesthetically.

It aligns especially well with:

- premium toy-house;
- felt/cardboard/soft handmade materials;
- domestic scale;
- gentle life-game tone.

## Animation fit

Excellent.

- floppy arm arcs are cheap and expressive;
- squash/stretch is expected;
- sitting and sleeping are extremely forgiving;
- objects can be hugged/held against the body instead of requiring finger grips.

## Interaction fit

Very strong.

Particularly good for:

- carry/hold;
- hug-sized recovered objects;
- sitting/resting;
- exaggerated pulling.

## Risks

- may feel like a manufactured plush toy rather than an inhabitant;
- can make the world feel too toy-box literal if every surface also uses soft craft materials;
- danger/anomaly scenes may lose tension if the character is excessively cuddly.

## Assessment

> Strong art-direction candidate, but slightly weaker world-mystery candidate than PEBBLE/SEED unless the material treatment remains ambiguous.

# 5. Direction D — Fold / Shell Resident

Working shorthand: `SHELL`

## Shape

- central soft body partially wrapped by one or two large folded shell/petal planes;
- limbs emerge from gaps;
- top silhouette can open/close slightly;
- standing silhouette is compact;
- sleeping/resting silhouette can close inward into a protected form.

Conceptual black silhouette:

```text
       /\
     /----\
    / (  ) \
  --|      |--
    \      /
     \____/
      |  |
```

The shell should be soft and rounded, not armor.

## World fit

Very strong narratively.

This body immediately suggests a creature adapted to an exposed room edge without needing exposition.

It can visually imply:

- self-protection;
- environmental adaptation;
- a relationship with boundary instability;
- residents who can make themselves compact when resting.

## Animation fit

Good, but more expensive than A/B/C.

- basic limb rig remains simple;
- shell/fold needs 1–3 extra control bones or blend shapes;
- opening/closing creates very strong emotional/body-language states.

## Interaction fit

Strong.

- arms remain available;
- edge sitting remains readable;
- inspection can open the shell slightly;
- sleep can close almost completely.

## Risks

- extra mesh deformation and self-intersection;
- shell can clip into chairs/desks/Atlas;
- more authoring/QA than the simplest body plans;
- if made too hard, it can drift toward armored alien design.

## Assessment

> Highest "worldbuilding through anatomy" value, with moderately higher production cost.

# 6. Direction E — Bell / Lantern Resident

Working shorthand: `BELL`

## Shape

- rounded bell / inverted-cup / hanging-lamp body;
- broad upper mass tapering toward short legs;
- arms emerge around mid-body;
- face is low on the body rather than on a separate head;
- one small top loop, nub, soft ring, or asymmetric cap can act as silhouette marker;
- optional restrained internal warm response/glow as an emotion/state channel.

Conceptual black silhouette:

```text
       __
      /  \
    /      \
   /        \
 --|        |--
   \________/
     |    |
```

The form must remain creature-like and handmade, not robot-like.

## World fit

Interesting but less naturalistic.

It can connect well to:

- Atlas resonance;
- signals;
- warmth/light;
- room-state reactions;
- a strange civilization whose inhabitants visually echo domestic objects without literally being objects.

## Animation fit

Very strong.

- low center joint complexity;
- whole-body wobble and ringing recoil can carry expression;
- sitting works through bottom-body contact;
- top cap/loop can trail movement.

## Interaction fit

Very strong.

Arms/legs are still standard stub-limb interaction points.

## Risks

- easiest candidate to drift into "cute robot" or "living lamp";
- may feel too directly tied to Atlas/technology;
- internal glow can violate the restrained-emissive art rule if overused.

## Assessment

> Visually distinctive experiment, but not the current safest foundation.

# 8. Four-player differentiation inside one body family

Regardless of the winning family, the first four players should not require four unrelated species or rigs.

Use the same base skeleton and body envelope, then vary a small number of high-readability channels.

Recommended channels:

- body hue/material tint;
- top silhouette marker;
- one secondary small side/back feature;
- eye shape or spacing;
- one accessory/socket item;
- idle personality timing.

Important rule:

> Do not make all four variants distinguishable only by color.

At least some variants should remain identifiable in black silhouette.

Possible marker families that do not change the main rig topology:

- single soft nub;
- paired folded ears/fins;
- short bent antenna;
- asymmetrical side fold;
- tiny crown/ridge;
- blunt sprout/bud;
- attached tag/ribbon-like flap.

Avoid long thin antennae that will alias/disappear at the fixed camera distance.

---

# 9. Animation-cost guardrails for final concept selection

A visually attractive concept should be rejected or simplified if it requires any of the following without a major gameplay reason:

- >2 extra locomotion limb chains;
- finger bones;
- long articulated tail;
- wings that must avoid room furniture;
- facial blendshape-heavy acting;
- cloth simulation for core identity;
- physics-driven tentacles for routine interactions;
- separate rig architecture for each player variant.

The point of replacing humans is to materially reduce interaction cost.

Do not reintroduce the same complexity under a more exotic silhouette.

---

# 10. Required future visual bake-off

Do not pick the final creature from text alone.

The next art/design test should compare at least PEBBLE and SEED (optionally SHELL) in a deliberately low-detail form.

Each concept must be judged in the actual frozen Office camera using the same tests:

1. standing idle beside a workstation;
2. walking through the central route;
3. sitting on an open floor edge with legs dangling;
4. holding one medium recovered object;
5. pulling a salvage line with visible effort;
6. reaching toward an Atlas control target;
7. curled/resting pose;
8. four-player lineup with silhouette markers;
9. black-silhouette screenshot;
10. 200 px-wide thumbnail readability.

Do not spend time on detailed textures, accessories, facial animation, or final materials before this test selects a body family.

---

# 11. Current conclusion

No final species is selected.

The current strongest design space is:

> **A compact soft resident that reads somewhere between a seed, pebble, handmade lump, and unknown organism — clearly alive, clearly non-human, physically capable of using tools, but difficult to classify as a familiar Earth creature.**

This direction best supports the project's broader world rule:

> familiar domestic/workshop forms coexist with things that are subtly impossible and not immediately explained.

Next decision after silhouette exploration:

- either perform a visual silhouette bake-off;
- or continue World Bible research into the stable rules of `the Outside / the Between` before final creature art is produced.

No Unity implementation is authorized by this document.
