# VQ2 Retrieval Orb — Visual Direction Update

> Date: 2026-09-01  
> Status: ACTIVE VISUAL DIRECTION / HUMAN REVIEW IN PROGRESS

## Current accepted constraints

- Retrieval Orb is the tether-end device for the Retrieval system.
- Most of the orb surface must be luminous; the glow is a primary visual/material domain, not a small central indicator.
- The orb must be visually attractive and premium enough to function as a memorable hero prop.
- The visual language should be futuristic, refined, warm, and compatible with the current Atlas / Retrieval Station world.
- Avoid magical-crystal, household-lamp, lantern, steampunk, industrial-hook, and generic sci-fi grenade reads.
- The top connection must read as a precise tether interface, not a decorative hanging ring.

## Rejected visual direction

The 2026-09-01 three-rib / cage-like skeletal frame direction is rejected by the user as too old-fashioned / conventional.

Do not continue with:

- three or four evenly spaced longitudinal ribs forming a cage;
- a cross-shaped or lantern-style frame wrapped around the sphere;
- heavy equatorial belts that divide the orb into obvious panels;
- symmetrical retro-futurist cage structures.

## New direction to explore

Use a more contemporary structural language while preserving a large uninterrupted luminous surface.

Preferred approach:

- near-spherical or very slightly vertically elongated luminous body;
- 70–85% of visible area remains continuous luminous material;
- structure is concentrated into a compact top tether module plus one or two asymmetric / floating precision elements rather than a full cage;
- consider recessed arcs, partial orbital rails, offset magnetic collars, flush embedded seams, or a suspended/floating mechanical ring that does not fully wrap the shell;
- minimal dark graphite and champagne-metal hardware;
- no decorative greeble density;
- science-fiction quality should come from proportion, material separation, precision interfaces, and controlled asymmetry rather than many exposed parts.

This direction remains subject to Human Visual approval before becoming final modeling reference.
