# VQ2 Chapter 1 / First Hour Narrative — 2026-09-03

Status: USER NARRATIVE DIRECTION — FIRST-HOUR CORE BEATS LOCKED / WAR IDENTITIES MAY REMAIN PERMANENTLY UNKNOWN

This document records the user's latest explicit first-hour narrative structure. It defines the intended opening experience and first major spectacle. The distant conflict is deliberately not required to resolve into named factions later; its identities may remain unknowable to the player for the entire game.

## 1. Arrival state

- The player is delivered into the refuge room through the existing left-side door by a machine / transfer mechanism.
- A small guide drone is already present in the room when the player arrives.
- The drone immediately has an onboarding role and helps the player understand the room through guided actions rather than a detached tutorial overlay.
- The player should begin with uncertainty about the larger cosmology, but should quickly learn enough practical room operation to function.

## 2. Initial supply crate

- A large supply crate / starter container is already present on the room floor at the beginning.
- It contains the player's initial usable supplies / materials.
- The crate is intended to support the first construction, furnishing, setup, repair, or activation tasks.
- The exact item list is not yet locked.

## 3. Initial room state

- The room is not completely empty at arrival.
- Some meaningful domestic and infrastructure objects already exist or are available very early.
- Current intended early/initial content includes examples such as:
  - wall-mounted wireless power hub / battery;
  - television / main display;
  - some desks / tables;
  - some chairs / seating;
  - basic living or operational equipment.
- The player is still expected to gradually build, furnish, improve, and personalize the refuge rather than receiving a fully completed luxury home.

## 4. First-hour onboarding intent

During roughly the first chapter / first hour, the player should be guided through practical actions that establish the game's basic verbs and room logic.

The drone may guide the player to:

- inspect the room;
- open the initial supply crate;
- understand or activate early infrastructure;
- place / use / restore some furniture or equipment;
- begin learning ordinary room life;
- begin understanding that the room is a survivable refuge rather than an ordinary apartment;
- encounter the basic logic of power, construction, and eventually Retrieval / Atlas without explaining the full cosmology.

The first hour should teach operational understanding before cosmological understanding.

## 5. First major outside spectacle — LOCKED CORE EVENT

Approximately within the first hour, the player should witness a distant but visually overwhelming conflict outside the refuge.

The intended sequence is:

1. From the room's open two-wall exterior view, the player can see very far into the surrounding external environment.
2. A group / fleet of spacecraft suddenly arrives at a distant location with an extremely fast transition, visually reminiscent of ships dropping out of high-speed / light-speed travel.
3. The fleet immediately attacks an indistinct or unreadable target / region.
4. The player cannot clearly identify what the fleet is firing at.
5. The fleet releases many bright beam / laser-like attacks into that area.
6. After a very short engagement, a single extremely powerful particle-like beam / energetic strike emerges from another location or source.
7. The strike annihilates the fleet with overwhelming speed.
8. The distant fleet erupts into a large-scale bright explosion / destruction event.

## 6. Relative power of the two sides — STRONG DIRECTION

- Both sides in the first-hour conflict should read as extremely advanced and powerful civilizations by human standards.
- The side capable of the annihilating strike is vastly stronger than the arriving fleet; the current user intuition is on the order of tens of times stronger, but the exact measurable ratio is not locked.
- The purpose of this asymmetry is to show the player that even a fleet that appears incomprehensibly advanced can still be insignificant relative to another civilization.
- This should expand the perceived scale of the higher-dimensional world without requiring explanatory exposition.

## 7. Audio and physical presentation — LOCKED INTENT

- The distant battle should initially be silent from the player's perspective.
- There should be no conventional immediate explosion sound traveling across vacuum-like exterior space.
- The visual effect should carry the spectacle first: beams, flashes, fleet destruction, and very strong distant light.
- After a noticeable delay, a physical disturbance / vibration reaches the refuge.
- The player should feel the event through the room / structure / Atlas field only after seeing it.
- The exact fictional transmission mechanism of this delayed vibration is not yet locked; it can later be explained through the refuge field, higher-dimensional substrate, structural coupling, or another original rule rather than ordinary sound propagation.

## 8. Narrative purpose of the event

This event should establish several things without exposition:

- the external environment is active, inhabited, and dangerous;
- powers exist outside that operate on a scale far beyond the player's room;
- the refuge is not located in an empty decorative starfield;
- the player is physically small relative to the larger conflict;
- survival inside the refuge does not mean the surrounding reality is safe or peaceful;
- the player does not yet understand the factions, target, weapon, or reason for the battle.

The event should create questions rather than answer them.

## 9. War identities — MAY REMAIN PERMANENTLY UNKNOWN

The game is not obligated to reveal the identities of the two civilizations involved in this first-hour battle.

It is acceptable — and currently preferred as a strong narrative option — for the player to finish the game without ever learning with certainty:

- who the arriving fleet belonged to;
- what they were attacking;
- who or what fired the annihilating strike;
- whether either side had any relationship to humanity's refuge;
- whether the engagement was offensive, defensive, preventive, accidental, or part of a larger war.

Later lore may provide context about civilization-scale conflict without retroactively naming this exact battle. The event can remain a piece of witnessed history larger than the player's own story.

## 10. Tone

The first hour should contrast ordinary domestic onboarding with enormous distant events.

The intended emotional progression is:

arrival -> practical uncertainty -> small domestic actions -> growing comfort -> look outward -> sudden distant war -> silent annihilation -> delayed physical vibration -> renewed uncertainty.

The scene should feel awe-inspiring and unsettling, not like an action mission the player is expected to join.
