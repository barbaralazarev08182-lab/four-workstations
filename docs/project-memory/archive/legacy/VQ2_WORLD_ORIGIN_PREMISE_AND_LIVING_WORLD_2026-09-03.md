# VQ2 World Origin Premise & Living World — 2026-09-03

Status: USER WORLD PREMISE — CORE DIRECTION WITH PROVISIONAL DETAILS

This document captures the user's latest explicit worldbuilding direction. It is not a complete lore lock. Items marked provisional remain open for later design.

## Core premise
- The Office / room should be understood as a small survivable living space separated from the destroyed home universe of humanity.
- The emotional inspiration is a protected small-universe / refuge-space idea: the ordinary universe outside humanity once knew is no longer safely inhabitable.
- Humanity's universe has been destroyed or otherwise lost.
- A non-human / alien species or group is responsible for providing the surviving human(s) with this small living space.
- The relationship is not yet to be framed simply as benevolent rescue. Their motives, limits, obligations, and reasons for preserving humanity are unknown.
- The player does not understand at the beginning why they are here, why this room exists, or why this alien group chose to preserve them.

## Opening / arrival
- The player enters the room through the existing door.
- The arrival should be machine-assisted: a machine or delivery / transfer mechanism sends the player into the room through that doorway.
- The room is therefore not merely a normal apartment reached through a corridor; the door is part of the mystery of arrival and transit.
- Early experience should begin from confusion and observation rather than a large lore explanation.

## Player identity
- The current humanoid player can for now be treated as human / human-derived for discussion.
- Exact biological status, restoration method, continuity of identity, and whether this is literally unchanged Homo sapiens remain open unless later locked.

## Companion life
### Cat — strong direction
- The room is planned to contain a cat.
- The cat should have very high interactivity and should materially contribute to the feeling that the room is lived in.
- The cat should not be treated as a static decorative prop.
- Future design should consider daily behavior, spatial habits, reactions to recovered objects / anomalies, furniture use, player affection / play, resting, eating, and possible world-discovery roles.

### Small guide drone — strong direction
- A small drone / compact robot companion is planned.
- It can serve as the in-world tutorial guide and onboarding helper.
- It should ideally teach through diegetic behavior rather than becoming a floating menu narrator.
- Its relationship to the alien group, the room, and the player is still open: caretaker, interpreter, monitor, local utility unit, or something more ambiguous.

## Outside / visible cosmos
- With first-person as the active gameplay view under evaluation, the player should be able to visually perceive an exterior cosmic environment beyond the room.
- The exterior can include intermittent distant events rather than a static star backdrop.
- Candidate exterior events / content include:
  - strange creatures passing in the distance, as solitary beings or groups;
  - drifting ruins or debris fields;
  - asteroid belts or unusual mineral formations;
  - alien craft belonging to, or potentially associated with, the group that provided the room;
  - large-scale silhouettes and rare distant phenomena.
- These exterior elements are currently worldbuilding candidates, not a fixed spawn catalog.

## Compatibility with existing Outside design
- The visible exterior may look cosmic, but its underlying physical rules do not need to be ordinary vacuum-space rules.
- Existing design principles can still remain valid: the room is a local stable frame; the boundary / Outside can have unusual distance, coupling, retrieval, and topology behavior.
- Therefore 'space' may be the player's visible interpretation of a stranger external medium rather than proof that conventional Euclidean astronomical space fully applies.

## Current narrative questions that should remain unanswered early
- Why was humanity preserved?
- Why this player?
- How many humans survived?
- Who built the room?
- Is the alien group still nearby?
- Are they observing the player?
- Is the guide drone fully independent?
- Why is Retrieval possible from the Outside?
- Are the distant creatures native to the same external environment?
- Is the room inside an alien vessel, attached to a larger structure, or maintained through another mechanism?
- Why is the door still present, and can it ever be used again?

## Design implication
The room should increasingly feel like a real home first and a cosmic refuge second. Ordinary life — drinking, eating, resting, listening to music, watching a screen, living with a cat — is not filler. It creates the normal baseline that makes the Outside, Retrieval, Atlas, strange recovered objects, and the truth of humanity's loss emotionally meaningful.

Do not explain the entire catastrophe in opening exposition. Prefer: ordinary domestic behavior -> small contradictions -> evidence -> larger interpretation.

## Inspiration boundary
The refuge-space premise is an inspiration point only. Do not copy characters, terminology, plot mechanisms, or distinctive story solutions from existing fiction. Build an original cosmology, alien motive, room logic, and reveal structure for VQ2.
