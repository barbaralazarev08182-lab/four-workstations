# Four Workstations / VQ2 — Scope Tiers & First-Five Product Promise

> Date: 2026-08-26  
> Status: PRODUCT SCOPE RECOMMENDATION — DISCUSSION / DECISION SUPPORT  
> Scope: classify current design into CORE / LAUNCH / LATER / MYSTERY RESERVE and identify the five experiences the first paid product must communicate  
> Parent review: `VQ2_MULTI_PERSPECTIVE_PRODUCT_AUDIT_2026-08-25.md`  
> This document does **not** authorize implementation. It is a scope-control artifact.

---

# 0. Why this document exists

The project no longer has an idea shortage.

The current risk is that too many strong concepts compete for equal importance, making the buyer promise harder to understand, the first playable experience slower to reach its strongest material, and the production scope unnecessarily broad.

Therefore the current design should be separated into four layers:

1. **CORE** — identity-level rules. If these are removed, the product becomes a different game.
2. **LAUNCH** — what the first paid release needs to make the CORE promise feel real rather than theoretical.
3. **LATER** — strong extensions that deepen the same product but are not required to prove the first release.
4. **MYSTERY RESERVE** — questions that should remain intentionally unresolved. They are not a feature backlog.

The product should stop adding new macro pillars until a vertical slice proves that the CORE and LAUNCH layers work together.

---

# 1. CORE — protect as product DNA

These are not necessarily all day-one mechanics. They are the rules that define what Four Workstations is.

## C1 — One persistent miniature room is the primary world

The room is not a disposable level, menu background, or lobby.

It should:

- persist across days;
- visibly accumulate state;
- become more capable and strange over time;
- remain the player's main emotional home;
- support solo play and 1–4 shared-room occupancy;
- deepen before the project solves retention mainly by adding unrelated maps.

If this is removed, the product loses its strongest ownership and continuity promise.

## C2 — Real-life effort changes the world, but does not monopolize agency

Focus / work / study remains a meaningful source of progression because it connects real life to the miniature world.

However:

> The player must still have meaningful choices and world interaction when they are looking at the game.

Do not make the enjoyable game hostage to a productivity timer.

Do not reduce Focus to an irrelevant `+Coins` button either.

The target relationship is:

`real effort -> visible world growth / progress / anticipation`

not:

`real effort -> permission to access the actual game`.

## C3 — Domestic Impossibility

The world must remain warm, tactile, miniature, domestic/workshop-like and visually understandable while impossible rules quietly emerge.

Examples:

- floor edges are normal lived space;
- residents sit with legs beyond the edge;
- something can be retrieved from beyond the room;
- ordinary-looking materials behave in subtly impossible ways;
- Atlas becomes extraordinary without becoming generic cyberpunk technology.

This contrast is a core brand and art-direction rule.

## C4 — Non-human inhabitants with a simple expressive body language

The residents should be visually distinctive, readable at the fixed miniature camera, and cheaper/more reliable to animate than realistic humanoids.

Current strongest body-plan direction remains:

> soft compact body/head mass + two stub arms + two stub legs + one strong silhouette marker.

Exact species remains open.

The characters are inhabitants of the world, not merely human office workers in costume.

## C5 — Low Verb / High Discovery

The player should learn the basic action language quickly.

Long-term novelty comes from:

- what appears;
- what changes;
- what reacts;
- what two old things suddenly do together;
- what later changes the meaning of an earlier discovery;
- what trace remains in the room.

Complexity belongs primarily in the world, not in control schemes, currencies, minigames, or deep menu hierarchies.

## C6 — The open edges are authored gameplay surfaces

At least two open boundaries should remain meaningful parts of the room rather than being visually closed for conventional safety.

Current strongest split:

- one edge = rest / watch / social presence;
- one edge = retrieval / salvage into the unknown.

The edges are part of the product silhouette and world grammar.

## C7 — The room remembers important events

Major events should often become persistent visual history.

Target progression:

`Before -> After -> History`

Examples:

- repaired crack remains visible;
- salvage hardware accumulates wear;
- an expansion seam remains legible;
- replaced Atlas parts can remain as memory objects;
- a past anomaly can create a future interaction surface.

If the world always resets to factory-new, the persistent-room promise is weakened substantially.

## C8 — Atlas is the long-term mythic object

Atlas should remain a central physical construction whose growth changes:

- the room silhouette;
- available capabilities;
- the player's understanding of reality.

It is not merely an upgrade station or multiplayer menu.

Its strongest long-term trajectory remains:

`construction -> response -> pattern -> echo -> stranger room -> mutual awareness -> later influence`.

## C9 — Shared without coercion

The room supports 1–4 real members.

Rules:

- solo must be a complete experience;
- additional players enrich/accelerate/leave traces;
- no essential progression should require all four online;
- no productivity leaderboard;
- no punitive absence / streak debt.

This preserves the original Four Workstations social fantasy without turning it into mandatory synchronous multiplayer.

## C10 — System scaling over content treadmill

The project should prefer:

`few reusable rules × objects × states × relationships`

over
`new month -> new furniture pack / new minigame / new currency / new map`.

New content should ideally connect to multiple existing systems.

# 2. LAUNCH — what the first paid release should actually prove

CORE is identity. LAUNCH is delivery.

A first paid release should not attempt to ship every idea in the current world bible. It should prove the following product loop convincingly.

## L1 — A lovable inhabited room from minute one

The player should immediately see:

- a warm miniature space;
- unusual resident creatures;
- two authored open edges;
- clear personal/shared zones;
- a central Atlas foundation;
- enough material/lighting quality that the room itself is desirable to inhabit.

The first impression should already show that this is not an ordinary office.

## L2 — Real-life Focus creates visible progress

The first release needs at least one satisfying proof that real-life effort changes the room.

This can feed:

- Atlas construction;
- a persistent world project;
- selected growth/state change;
- shared-room contribution history.

The reward must be visibly more meaningful than a number increment alone.

## L3 — Retrieval from the open edge

The player needs one signature active world action that is clearly non-office and visually distinctive.

Retrieval/salvage should provide a small curated set of outcomes rather than a large loot table.

The first launch set only needs enough content to prove that retrieved things can be:

- useful;
- strange;
- persistent;
- worth inspecting rather than automatically selling.

## L4 — A compact world-object ecology

Do **not** ship the whole 40+ candidate catalog.

Recommended first-release content target:

- roughly 5–8 strong world seeds;
- only the 3–4 rule families necessary to make those seeds meaningfully different;
- several ordinary recovered objects for contrast;
- at least one delayed relationship between old and new objects.

Strong initial candidates remain:

- Returning Piece;
- Closed Seed;
- Porcelain Moss;
- Indexed Joint;
- Listening Shell;
- one Paired Fragment relationship;
- Hairline Direction Crack + Repair Patch as one persistent-history chain.

The purpose is not quantity. The purpose is to prove that the player can say:

> “I remember that old thing — it matters now.”

## L5 — At least one permanent room-history chain

The first release needs a complete demonstration of:

`event -> player response -> persistent visible consequence -> later relevance`.

The Hairline Crack -> Repair -> Patch chain is currently the cleanest conceptual candidate.

Without this, “persistent room history” remains a design document claim rather than an experienced feature.

## L6 — Atlas must physically progress beyond decoration

Atlas should visibly move through multiple meaningful stages.

The first release must not end with Atlas functioning only as:

- an unfinished prop;
- a progress bar;
- a cosmetics sink.

It needs to demonstrate that construction leads to genuinely strange behavior: response, repeating pattern, directional behavior, or spatial echo.

## L7 — Recommendation: if Atlas/connected-world is marketed, ship first stranger-room observation

This is the most important scope fork.

### Preferred full-launch target

If the store page / trailer sells the idea that the room belongs to a larger network of real rooms, the first paid release should include at least:

> **one stable observation of the player's fixed stranger room through Atlas.**

It does **not** need full Ouroboros inference, incoming-observation mechanics, or influence yet.

But the buyer should not pay for a promise whose defining payoff exists only on a roadmap.

### Fallback if networking is not production-ready

If stranger-room observation cannot be made trustworthy for launch:

- do not market it as a shipped feature;
- keep the first release positioned around the local persistent strange-room loop;
- consider Early Access / Chapter-style framing only if appropriate;
- Atlas may end at an unexplained spatial echo, but the product promise must match that reality.

## L8 — 1–4 shared-room functionality must match the title/positioning

Because the project identity still includes Four Workstations and a persistent shared room, a full commercial launch should not casually ship as a purely solo product unless the title/positioning is deliberately changed.

However, networking depth should remain narrow:

- persistent shared room state;
- reliable member presence/history;
- contribution traces;
- no need for MMO-style synchronous systems.

## L9 — Curated placement, not full building-game freedom

Launch should favor:

- fixed/semantic personal slots;
- bounded placement surfaces;
- deterministic save/load/network state;
- reliable scene composition.

Broad free placement is not required to prove the product.

## L10 — Small economy, not economy as the game

Launch can contain:

- XP;
- Coins;
- limited Build Charge if it remains useful;
- physical objects;
- knowledge/state.

But players should experience world transformation before they experience a large store/catalog economy.

# 3. LATER — strong extensions that should not block the first proof

These ideas are valuable, but the product should earn the right to build them.

## LR1 — Full Ouroboros inference

- incoming vs outgoing relation;
- realization that the player is inside a larger chain;
- late evidence that the topology closes.

Keep as a major later revelation.

## LR2 — Costly cross-room influence

Later Atlas capability where one room can create a bounded event/opportunity/trace in another room.

Must preserve:

- target ownership;
- meaningful local cost/conservation;
- anti-spam / anti-harassment rules;
- visible consequence.

Do not block launch on this.

## LR3 — Large room expansion

- additional stabilized platforms;
- specialized side structures;
- observation deck;
- larger cultivation/research spaces;
- visually divergent room silhouettes.

Early launch only needs evidence that room-space can evolve, not a large construction sandbox.

## LR4 — Deep salvage tiers

- signal-targeted retrieval;
- containment retrieval;
- deep/high-risk recovery;
- rare cross-system objects;
- stronger ambition-linked consequences.

Launch only needs the simple signature form.

## LR5 — Deep cultivation ecology

- more growth families;
- surface-adapted variants;
- cross-system propagation;
- advanced useful/dangerous growth states.

Do not build a full farming economy.

## LR6 — Large item/content catalog

Additional objects should be added only after the first small ecology proves players care about relationships and delayed reinterpretation.

## LR7 — Broader customization and free placement

Unlock only after persistence/network placement is extremely trustworthy.

## LR8 — Advanced economy/prestige

- late Coin sinks;
- rare cosmetic commissions;
- optional shared projects;
- prestige room expression.

Exact numerical balancing belongs after the core world loop is fun.

## LR9 — More inhabitant body families/species

The first product should establish one iconic rig/body family first.

Species variety is not required for identity.

## LR10 — More maps / remote playable locations

Not forbidden forever, but should not become the primary solution to the small-room replayability problem.

# 4. MYSTERY RESERVE — intentionally do not answer yet

These are not missing features.

Their unresolved state creates value.

## M1 — What the Outside actually is

Do not collapse it early into:

- outer space;
- a void;
- a literal ocean;
- a named parallel dimension.

Its causal rules matter before its ontology.

## M2 — Why the rooms exist

Keep at least the Fracture and Organism/System interpretations alive early/mid game.

Do not choose a single encyclopedic answer just because design documents feel incomplete.

## M3 — Who/what created Atlas foundations

Keep open whether Atlas is:

- ancient architecture;
- repeated node anatomy;
- something residents partially rediscover;
- something else.

## M4 — Where recovered things truly come from

Not every object should be explained as:

- debris from another player's room;
- Atlas parts;
- ancient technology.

Multiple plausible origins should remain possible.

## M5 — Inhabitant origin

Do not explain the creatures as aliens, engineered workers, plant people, etc. during early product development.

Their lived behavior matters more than their biology.

## M6 — Why the ring topology exists

The Ouroboros shape should be discovered before its cause is explained.

## M7 — What happens conceptually if the chain is followed all the way around

This can remain a very-late mystery.

## M8 — Whether the ring itself can change

Insertion/removal is a backend requirement eventually, but the metaphysical meaning of topology changing should not be answered prematurely.

## M9 — Final cosmology / ultimate lore answer

Do not design toward a single reveal such as:

- “it was a simulation”;
- “it was a spaceship”;
- “an ancient corporation built everything.”

The product does not currently need an ultimate explanation to be coherent.

# 5. The extreme test — if the buyer only gets five things

If the first buyer can meaningfully remember only five experiences from the product, these should be the five.

## FIRST FIVE #1 — “I want to live in this little room.”

Immediate fantasy:

> A few small, unusual residents inhabit a warm miniature room that feels like home, workshop and tiny world at once.

The two open edges immediately communicate that the place is impossible but normal to its inhabitants.

This is the visual/emotional acquisition layer.

## FIRST FIVE #2 — “What I do in real life changes this place.”

The player completes real Focus/work/study and sees a physical project, object, growth or shared construction visibly progress.

This is the product's bridge between real life and game state.

## FIRST FIVE #3 — “I can pull something unknown into our room.”

At the open edge, the player retrieves something from beyond the room.

The object is not merely currency.

It may be useful, inert, reactive, alive-looking, or unexplained.

This is the first active curiosity engine.

## FIRST FIVE #4 — “This room remembers what happened here.”

A problem/event is resolved, but the room does not reset.

A patch, seam, object, growth, wear mark or other persistent consequence remains and may matter later.

This is the long-term ownership layer.

## FIRST FIVE #5 — “This room is not alone.”

Atlas gradually moves from strange construction to evidence of another persistent room.

Preferred full-launch payoff:

> the player eventually sees one fixed stranger room as a miniature world-state through Atlas.

This is the product's most distinctive long-term social/world revelation.

If this cannot ship, it must not be publicly promised as if it does.

---

# 6. What is deliberately NOT in the First Five

The following may exist, but they are not why a new customer should buy the game first:

- giant furniture catalog;
- deep shop/economy;
- crafting tree;
- daily quest checklist;
- multiple minigames;
- exact lore/cosmology;
- full Ouroboros topology explanation;
- cross-room influence;
- large base-building system;
- advanced research UI;
- broad free placement;
- multiple species;
- large map count.

If one of these starts consuming more production effort than the First Five before the First Five are excellent, scope is drifting.

# 7. Cut test — what happens if we remove something?

Use this as a practical scope audit.

## Can be cut from first release without destroying identity

- large shop;
- dozens of furniture items;
- deep crafting;
- full farming/cultivation loop;
- large expansion system;
- cross-world influence;
- complete ring explanation;
- large world-item catalog;
- full free placement;
- extra character species;
- many separate task types.

## Dangerous to cut

- persistent room;
- real-life effort -> visible world change;
- non-human inhabitants / strong visual identity;
- open-edge world grammar;
- retrieval of strange objects;
- persistent traces/history;
- Atlas as meaningful long-term object;
- solo viability;
- simple-hand/deep-world design philosophy.

## Conditional / positioning-sensitive

### Shared 1–4 room

If removed from full commercial launch while the product is still called/marketed as Four Workstations, the positioning becomes misleading or confused.

### Stranger-room observation

If marketed as a core feature, it must ship.

If deferred, the marketing must sell the local strange-room experience without implying a shipped global-room connection.

---

# 8. Recommended current stop rule

Until a vertical slice proves the First Five, do not add another macro pillar unless it solves a clearly identified failure in the First Five.

Allowed design work:

- improve the existing creature direction;
- improve the first salvage/content ecology;
- improve Atlas reveal pacing;
- improve persistent-history examples;
- improve buyer promise / visual identity;
- reduce production risk.

Default disallowed ideation for now:

- new major currencies;
- new independent activity genres;
- new maps as retention solution;
- new social graph concepts;
- new cosmological layers;
- new large crafting/farming/combat systems.

---

# 9. Recommended decision statement

The current project can be summarized as:

> **A persistent miniature world shared by up to four people, where real-life effort helps strange little inhabitants build, retrieve and investigate things, the room remembers what happened, and a mysterious central Atlas gradually reveals that this tiny home is connected to another real world.**

This is not final marketing copy.

It is a scope test.

Any major feature that does not strengthen this sentence should need a strong reason to enter the first release.

---

# 10. Next product-design question

The next useful question is no longer “what else can the world contain?”

It is:

> **What should the first 30–60 minutes prove, in what order, so that the player experiences all the seeds of the First Five without being overloaded or waiting days for the game to become interesting?**

That is an experience-sequencing problem, not a feature-invention problem.
