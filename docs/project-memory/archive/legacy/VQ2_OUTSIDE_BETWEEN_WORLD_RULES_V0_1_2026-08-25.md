# 0. Goal

The project needs consistent rules for what currently appears to be "outside the room".

Do **not** begin by answering the lore question:

> What is the Outside really?

Instead establish causal rules that make gameplay predictable while preserving mystery.

The Outside must explain, or at least remain compatible with:

- characters sitting safely at an open floor edge;
- objects being lowered/retrieved beyond that edge;
- some objects becoming lost or difficult to recover;
- rare or strange things returning from deeper retrieval;
- boundary anomalies and wall/floor instability;
- room expansion into previously open space;
- Atlas revealing a persistent stranger room;
- observation being detectable by the observed room;
- later cross-world influence requiring a meaningful cost;
- the global Ouroboros/ring topology without requiring rooms to be visibly arranged in a literal circle.

The Outside should create both everyday behavior and long-term mystery.

# 1. Research principles

External game/narrative design references support three methods that fit this system.

## 1.1 Curiosity should drive discovery

`Outer Wilds` is a strong reference for progression where the player explores because one mystery points toward another rather than because a task list dictates each step.

Relevant rule for Four Workstations:

> The Outside should first present observable facts, then let the player infer rules, and only later reveal partial explanations.

Example:

- first the player sees a resident sit safely at the edge;
- later the player loses a loose object over the edge;
- later a tethered object can be recovered;
- later a deeper rig behaves differently;
- only after repeated experience does the player understand that the edge is not a normal cliff.

## 1.2 Mundane + impossible is stronger than generic spectacle

`Control` is a useful reference because the Oldest House remains recognizably mundane while impossible spatial/world behavior becomes part of the setting.

Relevant rule:

> The room should remain warm, tactile, handmade, and domestic even when the rules around its boundaries become impossible.

Do not solve mystery by replacing the room with a conventional sci-fi visual language.

## 1.3 Systems should leave readable traces

Environmental-storytelling research emphasizes that players can infer history and rules through traces, reactions, damage, object placement, and persistent consequences.

Relevant rule:

> The Outside should leave evidence in the room.

Examples:

- residue after a risky retrieval;
- worn rig components;
- a patched edge;
- strange growth originating near a boundary;
- an Atlas module that changed after a detected signal;
- an object that remains visibly difficult to stabilize.

# 2. Strongest current premise

Current strongest proposal:

> The Outside is not an ordinary exterior location below or around the room. It is a **relational medium / boundary layer** in which normal distance, direction, and adjacency are weaker or different than inside a room-node.

The room is a **stable local frame**.

Inside that frame:

- down behaves consistently;
- furniture stays where it is placed;
- characters can walk normally;
- built structures have stable geometry;
- ordinary tools work predictably.

At the open boundary, the room's local rules gradually stop being sufficient.

Beyond it, objects are less strongly aligned with the room unless something continues to connect or anchor them.

This allows the room to feel ordinary locally while remaining embedded in something non-ordinary.

# 3. Proposed fundamental rules

These are the strongest proposed system rules. They are not yet final lore explanations.

## Rule 1 — Each room has a local stable frame

A room-node defines its own stable orientation and ordinary physical behavior.

Within the room's stable domain:

- the floor defines a reliable local "down";
- placed objects remain placed;
- characters move normally;
- construction behaves predictably;
- ordinary contact/weight is meaningful.

This is why the room can visually read as a normal miniature environment.

Important:

> The stable frame is a property of the node/world, not a visible force field.

It should usually be communicated through behavior rather than a sci-fi bubble effect.

---

## Rule 2 — The open edge is a transition, not a conventional cliff

The visible floor edge marks where the room's stable domain becomes weaker/less complete.

Therefore a creature can:

- sit on the edge;
- dangle its legs beyond it;
- lean over it;
- place a tool across it;

without the scene behaving like a normal deadly cliff.

The creature remains mostly anchored to the room through body contact.

This makes edge-sitting culturally ordinary while preserving uncertainty about what full separation would mean.

### Early-game gameplay guardrail

Routine movement should not accidentally kill/erase a resident because of the open edge.

Do not turn ordinary navigation into repeated falling punishment.

Exact in-fiction behavior if a resident fully leaves the stable frame remains open.

Possible later interpretations include:

- strong resistance/recoil before full detachment;
- residents being unusually node-bound;
- emergency recovery behavior;
- full crossing being impossible without special technology.

Do not lock one yet unless gameplay requires it.

---

## Rule 3 — Connection matters more than visual distance

A key proposed law:

> An object that remains physically or systemically connected to the room retains a stronger relationship to the room.

This is why a line, cable, arm, probe, tether, frame, or anchored structure matters.

Example:

```text
Room
  |
  | tether / line
  |
Unknown object
```

Because the tether remains attached to the node, the object has a recoverable path back.

A loose object crossing the boundary has no equivalent guarantee.

This gives salvage a clear fiction/mechanical foundation without requiring the Outside to be normal water or empty space.

---

## Rule 4 — "Depth" is not necessarily metric distance

Players may still use intuitive language such as:

- shallow;
- deeper;
- lower;
- further;

because that is how the interaction appears from the room.

But the system should treat "depth" primarily as:

> weaker correspondence / more difficult coupling to the home node.

Therefore a deeper salvage rig is not simply a longer rope.

It may require better:

- anchoring;
- sensing;
- stabilization;
- retrieval strength;
- containment;
- signal interpretation.

This lets upgrades unlock genuinely new classes of discoveries rather than only `+20m rope length`.

### Possible future tier language (working only)

- Near / Shore
- Drift
- Deep
- Seam

These names are placeholders, not lore locks.

---

## Rule 5 — Information crosses more easily than matter

This is one of the strongest proposed world rules.

> Patterns, signals, observations, or readable state can cross the larger structure more cheaply than physical influence.

This naturally explains Atlas progression:

1. detect a signal;
2. identify a pattern;
3. observe another node;
4. only much later create a physical/effective influence.

It also explains why:

- observation can unlock before interference;
- the target can detect coupling;
- actual influence requires a larger cost;
- the game can reveal another room before allowing item transfer or direct modification.

This should probably become a permanent rule unless later design strongly contradicts it.

---

## Rule 6 — Foreign matter can be more or less compatible with the room

Not every recovered object should behave identically after retrieval.

Some things integrate easily.

Others may:

- wobble;
- refuse to settle cleanly;
- emit residue;
- respond to Atlas;
- change material state;
- require containment;
- produce growth;
- remain partly "misaligned" with the local stable frame.

This gives Research / Inspect / Contain gameplay a fiction reason.

Important:

> Do not turn every foreign object into a hazard.

Some should be mundane, beautiful, funny, useless, or simply mysterious.

The unknown must contain calm discoveries as well as risky ones.

---

## Rule 7 — Risk should mostly be generated by forcing the boundary

World instability should correlate strongly with player ambition.

Examples:

- deeper retrieval;
- overloading a rig;
- forcing open an anomaly;
- processing an unstable object;
- extending a platform aggressively;
- stronger Atlas coupling;
- later cross-node influence.

This implements the existing principle:

> Risk follows ambition.

Avoid making the Outside a random punishment generator that damages the room simply because the player was absent.

Ambient strange events can still occur, but strong negative consequences should usually follow player-accepted risk.

---

## Rule 8 — Expansion means extending the stable frame

Future platforms should not be interpreted merely as ordinary floor tiles floating beside the Office.

Stronger proposal:

> Construction anchors new matter into the room-node and gradually extends the domain in which local room rules remain stable.

Therefore a major expansion project may require:

- structural material;
- one or more Anchor points;
- Build Charge / project effort;
- stabilization components;
- completion stages.

The reward is not only more square meters.

It is:

> "This place is now part of our world."

This gives physical expansion a much stronger narrative and economic meaning.

---

## Rule 9 — Topology is not the same as visible geography

The global ring:

`A -> B -> C -> ... -> N -> A`

should be understood as a relationship/topology, not necessarily a literal circle of rooms visible in one physical space.

Therefore:

- Room A can be topologically adjacent to Room B without seeing B across the edge;
- Room B can be linked onward to C;
- Atlas can discover the next relation even if normal visual distance does not apply;
- following the ring "all the way around" may be conceptually meaningful without requiring a physical map of rooms arranged in a circle.

This prevents the ring system from collapsing into ordinary geography.

---

## Rule 10 — Cross-world influence obeys conservation / consequence

Retain the World Bible proposal:

> Meaningful influence across node boundaries cannot be free.

Something local must be:

- consumed;
- transferred;
- destabilized;
- sacrificed;
- committed for a period;
- exposed to risk;
- or otherwise given up.

This gives influence weight and creates a durable late-game Sink.

Coins alone should not bypass the rule.

# 4. What normal edge behavior could look like

A resident approaches the open edge.

Inside the node:

```text
        resident
           O
          /|\
──────────/ \────────── floor
                     |
                     | edge
                     |
```

When sitting:

```text
        body remains supported
              O
             /|\
─────────────/──────── floor
             | |
             | |  legs beyond edge
             v v
          [Outside]
```

The body remains mechanically associated with the room because most of its support/contact is still in the stable frame.

This should feel ordinary to the inhabitant.

The player learns the rule through observation before receiving any explanation.

# 5. Salvage system implied by these rules

The Outside rules create a natural progression ladder.

## 5.1 Basic retrieval

Early tool:

- simple line / hook / probe;
- short operation;
- low instability;
- mostly common fragments and low-risk curiosities.

Player learns:

> connected things can come back.

## 5.2 Improved retrieval

Later rig:

- stronger anchor;
- better sensing;
- controlled reel/winch;
- ability to identify tension/response before committing;
- larger or less-compatible recoveries.

Player learns:

> "deeper" does not behave like ordinary distance.

## 5.3 Deep / risky retrieval

Later capability:

- special tether/material;
- stabilizer;
- containment vessel;
- voluntary risk decision;
- chance of tool damage, anomaly, residue, or difficult follow-up task.

Player learns:

> trying to pull more of the Outside into the room changes the room too.

# 6. Salvage result philosophy

Do not build the system as:

`cast line -> random item value -> sell`

Possible retrieval categories:

## A. Common useful matter

- ordinary components;
- worn parts;
- salvage material;
- repair inputs.

## B. Beautiful / collectible matter

- strange shapes;
- decorative fragments;
- harmless luminous/soft objects;
- room-history artifacts.

## C. Growth-capable matter

- seeds;
- spores;
- dormant organisms;
- crystals;
- objects that change in stages.

## D. Research objects

- unknown device fragment;
- repeating pattern;
- object with a response to Atlas;
- piece that implies another structure.

## E. Unstable matter

- difficult to anchor;
- leaves residue;
- changes local state;
- produces a follow-up situation.

## F. Traces rather than rewards

Some retrievals should mainly answer/ask world questions.

Examples:

- a material that matches the room wall exactly;
- a fragment that resembles another node's construction language;
- an object that appears older than the current room;
- two pieces that seem to have matching cut surfaces;
- something whose orientation changes near Atlas.

These are progression through knowledge rather than currency.

---

# 7. Anomalies implied by the Outside

Anomalies should feel like rule leakage, not arbitrary magic.

Useful anomaly families:

## Boundary mismatch

- hairline crack;
- edge surface behaving too soft/too rigid;
- object partly embedded in wall/floor;
- a shadow whose source is unclear.

## Alignment mismatch

- object refuses to settle;
- a small item slowly rotates despite no force;
- temporary local direction disagreement;
- loose props drift slightly near one zone.

## Material mismatch

- residue;
- growth;
- condensation-like impossible substance;
- a recovered object changes after entering the room.

## Signal mismatch

- Atlas reacts before activation should be possible;
- one wall surface repeats a pattern;
- another room's state seems to leave a faint trace.

Important tone rule:

> Strange does not automatically mean hostile.

Most anomalies should first invite curiosity.

---

# 8. Atlas under these rules

The Atlas system becomes much more coherent if it is understood as a machine that manipulates **correspondence between nodes**, not as a portal generator.

## Stage A — Local construction

Atlas is only a strange shared project.

## Stage B — Sensing

It detects structured response in the external medium.

## Stage C — Address / Pattern

It identifies one persistent relation rather than random noise.

## Stage D — Observation

Information/state from the adjacent node becomes legible.

The observed node experiences detectable coupling.

## Stage E — Stable observation

The miniature becomes clearer/more persistent.

## Stage F — Influence

A local cost is committed and some limited effect crosses the relation.

This structure directly follows:

`information is easier to cross than matter/effect`

and

`influence obeys conservation`.

# 9. Why the observed room knows it is being watched

The current design requirement is:

- target knows observation is happening;
- target does not know observer identity.

Under the proposed Outside rules:

> Observation is a real coupling state between nodes.

Therefore the target node can feel/react to the fact that its correspondence is active.

It does **not** automatically receive identity metadata.

Possible diegetic signals remain open:

- Atlas component rotates;
- a material warms;
- a small object resonates;
- light subtly shifts;
- a quiet mechanical/organic sound;
- a dedicated in-world indicator.

The important thing is causal consistency:

> detectable connection != known observer identity.

# 10. Room expansion under these rules

A future expansion sequence could be:

```text
Select boundary project
-> install anchor frame
-> contribute materials / Build Charge
-> stabilize the frame
-> first temporary platform appears
-> local room behavior extends onto it
-> platform becomes permanent part of node
-> new behavior/facility becomes possible
```

This is stronger than:

`pay Coins -> floor tile appears`.

It also explains why expansion can be visible in stages.

Possible visual stages:

- probe stakes / frame;
- suspended incomplete beams;
- unstable translucent/unfinished floor concept;
- finished physical material;
- installed facility.

Exact visuals remain open.

# 11. What the Outside should NOT become

## Not normal outer space

Avoid default black starfield, planets, spacecraft language, and generic cosmic neon.

## Not simply an ocean reskin

Fishing-like input is useful, but the Outside should not secretly function as normal water with renamed fish.

## Not a constant death pit

Open edges are part of domestic life.

Routine pathing should not create annoying fall-death loops.

## Not an infinite loot faucet

Deep retrieval must introduce cost, uncertainty, capacity limits, wear, research requirements, or risk.

## Not arbitrary magic

The player should gradually be able to predict categories of outcome from learned rules.

Mystery is not the same as inconsistency.

## Not a lore-dump target

Do not introduce a terminal that immediately explains:

- what the Outside is;
- who created it;
- why the ring exists;
- where every artifact comes from.

The world should remain partly interpretable through action and evidence.

# 12. Visual direction for the Outside

The exact rendering is open, but the following principles are recommended.

## Desired

- quiet;
- depth without clear normal horizon;
- soft haze / layered translucency / low-frequency movement;
- occasional suspended traces/threads/fragments;
- impossible but restrained parallax;
- subtle reaction near active tethers or Atlas coupling;
- warm room remains visually dominant.

## Avoid

- constant dramatic particles;
- bright neon grid;
- obvious galaxy backdrop;
- large moving cosmic objects that steal the miniature-room identity;
- horror darkness as the default state.

The emotional baseline should be:

> calm enough to sit beside, strange enough to wonder about.

This is one of the most important tone tests.

# 13. World-rule-to-gameplay map

| World rule | Visible player behavior | System value |
|---|---|---|
| Room has stable local frame | ordinary furniture / walking works | keeps room readable/domestic |
| Edge is transition, not cliff | sit/dangle safely | creates signature resting behavior |
| Connection preserves relation | tethered salvage returns | gives retrieval logic |
| Depth = weaker coupling | deeper rigs unlock new discovery classes | supports progression without normal map travel |
| Information crosses easier | Atlas observes before influence | explains staged multiplayer unlock |
| Foreign matter has compatibility | inspect/research/contain | creates non-currency progression |
| Forced boundary use creates strain | optional risky tasks | meaningful consequence system |
| Expansion extends stability | build new platforms | physical long-term progression |
| Topology != geography | persistent stranger ring | supports Ouroboros without literal room circle |
| Influence requires conservation | paid cross-world effects | late-game sink / anti-spam |

# 14. What the player should learn, in order

A possible discovery order:

## Early

1. The edge is safe enough to sit on.
2. Loose and connected things behave differently near it.
3. A tether can recover something that was not visible before.

## Early-mid

4. Not all recovered things behave normally in the room.
5. Better equipment reaches qualitatively different results.
6. Forcing deeper interaction can damage or destabilize local systems.

## Mid

7. The room can extend its own stable area outward.
8. Atlas detects structured signals, not random noise.
9. One signal is persistent.

## Mid-late

10. The persistent signal belongs to another real room.
11. Observation itself is detectable there.
12. The connection appears to be one link in a larger chain.

## Late

13. Effects can cross, but only at meaningful cost.
14. The ring/topology has deeper properties the player still does not fully understand.

This lets curiosity itself remain progression.

# 15. Open questions — deliberately unresolved

Do not answer these yet unless later mechanics require it.

- Is the Outside one continuous medium shared by every room?
- Is "depth" experienced the same way from every edge?
- Are retrieved objects from old rooms, active rooms, nowhere identifiable, or multiple sources?
- Can two different rooms ever retrieve related pieces of the same object?
- Why are inhabitants apparently safe near the boundary?
- Are inhabitants inherently node-bound?
- Can a resident ever deliberately cross completely?
- Does the ring exist inside the Outside, underneath it, or only as a topological relation?
- Can ring adjacency ever change?
- Does building outward merely extend one node, or can new sub-nodes form?
- Can the Outside remember past activity?
- Can influence leave long-lived traces along the chain?
- Is the Outside natural, engineered, biological, architectural, or something these categories cannot describe?

Preserve ambiguity where it creates useful long-term mystery.

# 16. Current strongest design package

The most coherent current package is:

> **The room is a stable local world-node. Its exposed edges are transitions into a relational medium where normal distance/direction weaken. Physical connection preserves recoverability; deeper interaction means weaker correspondence rather than simply greater distance. Information crosses more easily than matter, which is why Atlas can observe before it can influence. Expansion anchors new space into the room's stable frame. Strong boundary manipulation creates risk, and cross-world influence must obey a conservation/cost principle.**

This package explains a large number of previously separate gameplay ideas with a small set of repeatable laws.

No final lore name for the Outside is selected.

No Unity implementation is authorized by this document.
