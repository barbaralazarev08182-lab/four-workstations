# 0. Purpose

Atlas should not be designed as a normal upgrade building with a hidden multiplayer menu inside it.

Its strongest role is:

> **the room's long-term mythic object: a shared construction whose physical growth gradually reveals that the room belongs to a larger, persistent, anonymous world structure.**

The player should not know Atlas's full function at the beginning.

The intended emotional sequence is:

`ordinary construction -> curious response -> repeatable pattern -> impossible echo -> another real room -> persistent stranger -> awareness of being observed -> proof of a larger chain -> costly influence`

Atlas should therefore carry three forms of progression at once:

1. **Physical progression** — the object visibly becomes more complete.
2. **Capability progression** — new actions become possible.
3. **Knowledge progression** — the player's model of the world changes.

The third category is as important as the first two.

---

# 1. Existing constraints that Atlas must preserve

The following are current design constraints and should not be silently replaced:

- Atlas is gradually built in the central area between the four workstations.
- Solo play must remain viable; four simultaneous players are not required.
- Multiple room members may contribute.
- Physical growth must be visible in the scene rather than represented only by a progress bar.
- Another player's room is not visible at the beginning.
- The first cross-room capability is observation only.
- The linked stranger room is assigned without a friend-selection relationship and then remains persistent/fixed.
- The observed room knows when observation is occurring, but does not know the observer's identity.
- Later progression may unlock some form of influence.
- Influence must have a meaningful price/cost/risk/opportunity cost.
- The global room topology is Ouroboros-like / closed-chain-like.
- Do not assume conventional gifting, chat, griefing, PvP, direct object editing, or friend-list behavior.
- Atlas must remain compatible with the warm handmade miniature-world art direction; no default cyberpunk portal/hologram treatment.

---

# 2. External design lessons applied

## 2.1 Curiosity should unlock the next question

Outer Wilds' curiosity-driven structure is useful as a design lesson: progression can come from inference and accumulated knowledge rather than only explicit mission rewards.

Application to Atlas:

- do not explain Atlas before the player has observed its behavior;
- each Atlas stage should answer one question and generate another;
- the player's desire to continue building should increasingly come from wanting to understand what the object is doing.

## 2.2 The environment/system itself should carry narrative

Environmental-storytelling practice supports using world reaction, object state, material change, sound, layout, and interactive systems to communicate history and rules.

Application to Atlas:

- Atlas should teach through physical response;
- avoid long exposition dumps explaining every stage;
- a misaligned ring returning to the same orientation can be more powerful than a text box saying “remote link detected.”

## 2.3 Asynchronous social contribution should change useful world structure

Death Stranding is a strong reference for social systems in which players contribute resources to persistent structures that materially improve or change what people can do without requiring conventional direct multiplayer interaction.

Application to Atlas:

- contributions should physically alter Atlas;
- later social meaning should emerge through persistent traces and capabilities;
- social value should not depend on chat, friend requests, or synchronous co-presence.

These are design-method references, not visual or lore templates.

# 3. Why do inhabitants start building Atlas?

This is a crucial problem. The weakest version is:

> “A quest says: Build Atlas to unlock multiplayer.”

That destroys much of the mystery.

Three fiction structures were considered.

## Option A — The inhabitants already know what Atlas is

They deliberately build a known communication machine.

Advantages:

- easy onboarding;
- clear goals;
- simple tutorial writing.

Problems:

- removes discovery;
- makes the later stranger-room reveal expected rather than astonishing;
- turns Atlas into a conventional technology tree.

Current assessment: **too literal**.

## Option B — Atlas begins as an unknown central foundation that accepts recovered parts

Strongest current proposal.

The room contains or exposes a strange central base/foundation that does not behave like ordinary furniture.

The inhabitants may know how to maintain the room, but they do not know the base's full purpose.

At some point, a recovered object / component / fragment is brought near it and produces an unmistakable fit or response.

The player learns:

> “This thing was meant to accept something.”

Additional components progressively form a structure.

The motivation to continue changes over time:

`it fits -> it reacts -> it repeats -> it points -> it shows something -> someone is there`

This allows curiosity itself to become a driver.

## Option C — Atlas is designed from scratch by the residents

The residents invent it after studying the Outside.

Advantages:

- supports a strong workshop/craft identity;
- makes player agency central.

Problems:

- requires more explanation for why an invented machine happens to discover the exact ring topology;
- can feel less mysterious unless some deeper world structure still guides the design.

Current assessment: useful as a partial truth, but weaker than Option B as the initial fiction.

## Current recommendation

Use a hybrid centered on Option B:

> **The room contains a pre-existing or not-fully-understood central foundation. Residents do real construction work around it, but they are completing / interpreting something whose deeper behavior they did not invent.**

This keeps both agency and mystery.

Do not yet decide whether the original foundation is ancient architecture, a biological/system organ, a repeated node feature, or something else.

# 4. Physical identity of Atlas

Atlas should begin materially humble and become extraordinary without visually turning into generic science fiction.

Recommended material language:

- warm muted metal;
- wood / composite blocks;
- ceramic or off-white structural pieces;
- frosted/translucent material in small amounts;
- brass-like connectors;
- paper/blueprint/tool traces in early phases;
- limited slow mechanical motion;
- restrained light only after later stages.

Avoid:

- giant glowing portal ring;
- blue hologram table;
- laser beams as default language;
- floating sci-fi HUD panels;
- glossy black futuristic machinery;
- hard-edged spaceship console design.

Atlas should feel closer to:

> **a handmade instrument / architectural model / strange communal machine whose impossible function slowly emerges.**

## 4.1 Four-sided structure

Atlas can retain four local contribution faces/modules oriented toward the four workstations.

These faces need not mean “four keys required.”

They can visually record who has contributed in the current stage without blocking solo completion.

This preserves:

- room-member identity;
- visible shared ownership;
- 1–4 player compatibility.

# 5. Atlas progression ladder — narrative + capability

The following stages are a **design ladder**, not a locked implementation count. Multiple stages may later be merged for production.

## Stage 0 — THE BASE / Quiet Foundation

Player-facing state:

- a low central foundation, recess, plate, socket, or incomplete structure exists;
- it does not present a readable purpose;
- early tools/parts may surround it.

Knowledge gained:

- almost nothing.

Question created:

> Why is this central thing here?

Gameplay role:

- provides a visible long-term construction target;
- can accept the first meaningful component later.

Important tone:

It should not look like a broken portal.

It may simply look oddly specific compared with ordinary furniture.

---

## Stage 1 — FIRST FIT / Assembly

Trigger concept:

A suitable recovered/constructed component is brought to the base and fits in a way that feels too exact to be coincidence.

Physical change:

- first structural frame;
- one or more radial sockets become legible;
- tools/blueprints/parts shift from clutter to intentional construction.

Knowledge gained:

> The base is part of something larger.

Question created:

> Larger *what*?

Gameplay unlock:

- contribution / construction loop becomes explicit;
- no remote-world function yet.

---

## Stage 2 — RESPONSE / It Notices

Physical change:

- a restrained moving part appears;
- a ring, needle, weighted arm, shutter, or suspended piece can move slightly;
- one material surface may become faintly warm/luminous only during response.

World behavior:

Atlas reacts to specific successful construction, recovered material, calibration, or room events.

The response is repeatable but not yet understood.

Knowledge gained:

> This is not only a passive structure.

Question created:

> What is it responding to?

Design rule:

Avoid immediately displaying readable text such as `SIGNAL DETECTED`.

Let the player first see the response.

---

## Stage 3 — PATTERN / It Points Somewhere That Is Not a Direction

Physical behavior:

- one moving element repeatedly returns to the same orientation/configuration even when manually displaced;
- different recovered objects may cause related responses;
- a recurring pulse/rhythm/shape appears.

This is where the previously defined Outside rule becomes useful:

> Room-to-room relationship is topological, not conventional geographic distance.

Therefore Atlas's “orientation” does not need to point north/east/down.

It can instead stabilize into a recurring configuration that the inhabitants initially mistake for direction.

Knowledge gained:

> The responses are structured, not random.

Question created:

> What is on the other side of this pattern?

Gameplay unlock candidates:

- calibration tasks;
- compare recovered objects against Atlas response;
- first archive/notebook entries.

---

## Stage 4 — ECHO / Something Has Shape

Major transition.

Atlas begins producing a spatial echo that is clearly more than light/noise but not yet a recognizable room.

Possible physicalized presentation:

- small frosted/ceramic/translucent volumes rise, unfold, or assemble over the center;
- the volumes form incomplete floor planes, walls, or object-like masses;
- parts appear/disappear as the signal stabilizes.

Do **not** default to a flat monitor feed.

The important player reaction is:

> “That looks like a place.”

not yet:

> “Oh, that's another player's room.”

Knowledge gained:

> The pattern contains spatial structure.

Question created:

> Is Atlas reading somewhere else?

---

## Stage 5 — WINDOW / The Stranger Room

This is the first major world revelation.

Atlas stabilizes enough to present a coherent miniature of one persistent stranger room.

Rules:

- same linked room persists across sessions/days;
- it is not a daily random recommendation;
- it is not chosen from the user's friend list;
- it represents another real room state;
- observation is the only cross-room capability at first;
- the target knows that observation is occurring;
- observer identity is not revealed.

### Recommended presentation principle

The miniature should represent **world state**, not feel like a surveillance camera feed.

It may show:

- room structure;
- major constructed facilities;
- Atlas state;
- expansion silhouette;
- visible resident presence/actions at an appropriate abstraction level;
- persistent room changes;
- unusual events.

Avoid designing it as a literal webcam or unrestricted live video metaphor.

This improves:

- tone;
- privacy feeling;
- technical scalability;
- readability at miniature scale;
- mystery.

### Target awareness

When A actively observes B, B's own world should show a diegetic reaction.

Possible categories:

- Atlas ring shifts;
- indicator/material warms;
- small mechanical response;
- sound tone;
- one subtle in-world signal.

The target learns:

> “Someone is looking through the chain.”

but not:

> “It is Room A / user X.”

Question created for the observer:

> Why this same room every time?

Question created for the target:

> Who is on the other side?

---

## Stage 6 — FAMILIARITY / A Stranger Becomes Recognizable

This is not primarily a new technical feature. It is a social/narrative progression stage created by persistence.

Over repeated observation, the player begins recognizing:

- that room's layout;
- recurring resident habits;
- ongoing construction;
- strange events;
- changes since last observation;
- perhaps specific objects or cultivation projects.

The key emotional design goal:

> The stranger should become familiar without becoming a conventional friend-list contact.

Possible system support:

- Atlas emphasizes **change since last observation** rather than offering endless feed content;
- room history may leave subtle visual traces;
- no forced identity reveal is required.

Knowledge gained:

> This is a persistent neighbor, not random content.

Question created:

> If I always see the same room, what determines the relationship?

---

## Stage 7 — COUNTERTRACE / Someone Is Also Looking at Us

The room has already established that observation is detectable by the target.

A later Atlas upgrade can make this more legible from the player's own side.

Example:

- the local Atlas develops a separate response that does **not** match the outgoing observation state;
- the room realizes it can be observed from another connection while it observes its own fixed target.

This is a powerful way to introduce directed-chain structure without showing a social graph.

Possible conceptual result:

`Unknown predecessor -> Your Room -> Persistent observed room`

The identities remain hidden.

Knowledge gained:

> We are not simply looking outward. We are one position inside a larger structure.

Question created:

> How many positions are there, and does this structure ever return to itself?

Important:

Do not immediately display a full ring map.

Let players infer topology from behavior first.

---

## Stage 8 — CONSERVATION TEST / Influence Has Weight

Before real cross-room influence becomes available, Atlas should teach the conservation rule experimentally.

A controlled attempt to make the remote miniature react produces a local cost/transfer/instability.

The exact resource is open.

Possible forms:

- a specific item is consumed;
- a prepared material changes state;
- Atlas must discharge/recover;
- a local opportunity is sacrificed;
- temporary instability appears;
- a rare input is irreversibly transferred.

The key lesson is diegetic:

> Seeing another room is relatively easy. Causing something to cross the relationship is not.

Knowledge gained:

> Influence obeys a price/conservation rule.

Question created:

> What is worth sending across?

This stage is critical for making later economy sinks feel like world law instead of balance taxes.

---

## Stage 9 — INFLUENCE / Leave a Trace

Later progression unlocks one tightly bounded influence action.

Do not begin with “edit another player's room.”

The strongest design class is:

> **cause a bounded world event/opportunity/trace in the target room while preserving the target player's ownership and agency.**

Possible categories for future study, not approved content:

- create a temporary anomaly that can be investigated;
- cause a recoverable object/opportunity to appear;
- affect a non-owned environmental state for a limited time;
- accelerate or alter one eligible world process;
- leave a persistent but non-destructive trace;
- create a choice for the target rather than forcing a permanent change.

Avoid by default:

- deleting/moving target-owned objects;
- draining target resources;
- spam effects;
- irreversible sabotage;
- anonymous harassment.

Influence should be:

- expensive enough to matter;
- visually obvious enough to notice;
- bounded enough to remain safe;
- strange enough to fit the world;
- open-ended enough to create stories.

---

## Stage 10 — OUROBOROS INFERENCE / The Larger Shape

The ring should become a world revelation, not merely backend architecture.

The player does not need a literal map of every room.

Possible evidence:

- distinct incoming/outgoing resonance signatures;
- repeated structural rules across observed rooms;
- recovered/recorded patterns implying exactly one onward relation;
- an Atlas upgrade that compares chain signatures without revealing identities;
- very late evidence that following the relation eventually closes rather than extending infinitely.

The important realization is:

> “Our room is not connected to the network. Our room is *inside the shape of the network*.”

Whether the inhabitants ever prove the full ring mathematically is still open.

# 6. Recommended pacing of reveals

Do not make Atlas construction stages all equally spaced or equally dramatic.

A useful emotional rhythm is:

### Early: frequent physical rewards

The player should quickly see parts being added.

Purpose:

- establish construction satisfaction;
- make Atlas worth caring about before mystery deepens.

### Middle: fewer but stranger rule revelations

Responses become less about “more metal” and more about “this thing behaves impossibly.”

Purpose:

- shift motivation from completion to curiosity.

### Observation unlock: major chapter break

The first coherent stranger room should be rare enough to feel earned.

It should not happen so late that players never see the unique social premise, but it should also not happen on day one.

Exact real-time pacing remains a playtest/balance question.

### Late: capabilities become expensive and consequential

Influence should arrive after the player already cares about the persistent stranger and understands the world's conservation rule.

Do not unlock expensive influence before the relationship has emotional/contextual meaning.

---

# 7. The first stranger-room reveal — recommended sequence

This moment deserves deliberate staging.

Avoid:

`Build reaches 100% -> popup: Stranger Room Unlocked -> click button`

Recommended diegetic sequence:

1. Final required part/calibration is completed.
2. Atlas reacts, but does not immediately explain itself.
3. A familiar pattern from earlier stages becomes unusually stable.
4. Small spatial forms begin appearing over the center.
5. The player recognizes a floor plane.
6. Then walls / furniture masses.
7. Then one detail that cannot belong to the player's own room.
8. Eventually a small living resident or active room state becomes visible.
9. The player realizes this is not a generated diagram of their own room.
10. Only after the visual realization does minimal UI/system language confirm that an external room has been reached.

The emotional goal:

> **recognition before explanation.**

The player should have a brief moment of “wait… is that someone else's place?” before the game labels the feature.

# 8. Atlas and the three-resource economy

The current economy skeleton remains:

- XP — permanent personal growth;
- Coins — common/predictable spending;
- Build Charge — behavior-bound shared-project contribution resource.

Atlas should prevent Coins from becoming a universal shortcut.

## 8.1 Core Atlas progress

Recommended requirement classes:

`Build Charge + construction work + selected discoveries/materials`

not:

`Coins -> instant Atlas level`

Coins may still support ordinary needs:

- standard fasteners/parts;
- routine repair;
- optional cosmetic finish;
- workshop service/conversion costs.

But core world revelation should require actual play/discovery.

## 8.2 Discovery gates

Some Atlas stages may require a specific world discovery rather than a numeric threshold.

Examples of gate *types*:

- recover a component that resonates with the base;
- research a pattern;
- stabilize an anomaly;
- complete a calibration experiment;
- identify a repeated signal.

This prevents the main mystery from collapsing into pure resource grinding.

## 8.3 Use-driven costs, not offline decay

Atlas should not demand arbitrary daily maintenance simply because the user did not log in.

Better recurring sinks:

- heavy observation mode / experiments;
- calibration after risky use;
- influence attempts;
- repair after deliberate overload;
- processing special materials.

Activity should generate both opportunities and expenses.

# 9. Atlas tasks should evolve with its meaning

Early Atlas tasks can be concrete:

- assemble frame;
- install connector;
- sort parts;
- fit component.

Middle tasks should become investigative:

- compare responses;
- rotate/alignment test;
- expose a recovered object to the mechanism;
- identify recurring pattern;
- stabilize a spatial echo.

Observation-era tasks should become relational:

- observe change in linked room;
- study why observation is detectable;
- compare incoming/outgoing Atlas responses;
- infer chain properties.

Late tasks can become consequential:

- prepare a cross-link experiment;
- choose what to sacrifice/input;
- accept risk;
- observe the result in another room;
- recover from local instability if the experiment fails.

This keeps Atlas from becoming a finished decorative monument after its first major unlock.

---

# 10. What makes the same stranger worth observing repeatedly?

Atlas should not imitate a social-media feed.

The value comes from **persistence + change + partial knowledge**.

The same room becomes interesting because the observer gradually recognizes:

- “they expanded that edge”; 
- “that strange growth is bigger”; 
- “their Atlas changed”; 
- “someone is active today”; 
- “yesterday that wall was normal”; 
- “they seem to keep one odd object on the same shelf”; 
- “their room keeps developing in a direction different from ours.”

Design principle:

> The system should help players notice meaningful change, not maximize content volume.

A small number of persistent visible differences can create more attachment than an infinite carousel of random rooms.

---

# 11. Observation privacy/tone principle

Because the target knows observation is occurring, the mechanic should feel like **mutual world contact**, not hidden surveillance.

Recommended tone:

- mysterious;
- slightly intimate;
- socially present;
- non-invasive;
- anonymous but not covert.

The miniature abstraction is helpful here.

The player is reading another **world-state representation**, not opening a security camera.

Exact synchronized state remains an implementation/product decision later.

---

# 12. Atlas failure / risk design

Normal construction should be forgiving.

Stronger risk should appear when the player voluntarily pushes beyond stable operation.

Possible future risk actions:

- over-amplify a weak echo;
- force an incomplete calibration;
- test an unstable recovered component;
- attempt influence without ideal stabilization;
- keep Atlas coupled longer than recommended.

Possible consequences:

- local Atlas misalignment;
- temporary room anomaly;
- damaged replaceable component;
- lost experiment input;
- distorted observation;
- follow-up repair/recovery task.

Avoid making normal inactivity or ordinary observation destroy progress.

Failure should preferably create world-state content rather than a generic currency penalty.

---

# 13. Spatial consequences for Office design

Atlas needs more than its mesh footprint.

Reserve:

- central build footprint;
- four-sided contribution/approach positions;
- operating stance(s) for creature characters;
- clear miniature-readability volume above Atlas;
- room-camera visibility;
- route clearance;
- short construction-effect clearance;
- later observation state readability;
- later influence/overload visual clearance.

Its growth should remain primarily vertical / layered / articulated rather than expanding sideways until it blocks the four-workstation composition.

The exact live Unity dimensions must later be measured from the current scene, not copied from old Web coordinates.

# 14. Art-direction progression

Atlas's strange nature should emerge gradually.

## Early

- matte materials;
- screws/frames/plates;
- paper/tool traces;
- no major glow;
- clearly handmade.

## Response stage

- one subtle moving component;
- restrained material warmth;
- tiny repeated sound/kinetic cue.

## Echo stage

- physicalized miniature fragments;
- frosted/translucent volumes;
- spatial form is more important than brightness.

## Observation stage

- coherent miniature room;
- enough movement/state change to read as alive;
- Atlas still feels like an object inside the warm room, not a portal replacing the room.

## Influence stage

- stronger but bounded reactions;
- visible local cost/consequence;
- avoid escalating into generic sci-fi spectacle.

---

# 15. Mystery questions Atlas should deliberately preserve

Even after observation unlocks, do not immediately answer:

- Who originally made the central foundation?
- Does every room begin with the same foundation?
- Why are rooms linked in this exact order?
- Is the observed room physically “next” to ours in any normal sense?
- Does Atlas discover the ring, restore it, or participate in creating/stabilizing it?
- Why does observation create a detectable reaction?
- Where does the conservation cost actually go?
- Can a room's position in the chain ever change?
- Is there a meaningful first/last node if the topology is closed?
- What would happen if the chain were somehow interrupted?

These questions are long-term narrative fuel.

---

# 16. Current strongest recommendation

The strongest current Atlas concept is:

> **A pre-existing, incomplete central foundation gradually completed by the room's residents using real construction effort and strange discoveries. At first it merely fits pieces; then it responds; then it reveals a structured signal; then an impossible spatial echo; finally it resolves into the miniature state of one persistent stranger room. Continued progression reveals that observation is reciprocal/detectable, that the room occupies one directed position in a much larger closed chain, and that any true influence across that relationship requires a meaningful local price.**

This structure makes Atlas simultaneously:

- the room's primary shared construction;
- the bridge between economy and world mystery;
- the gateway to the game's asynchronous social premise;
- a long-term sink for meaningful resources/actions;
- a source of new gameplay verbs;
- the main device through which the player discovers the world is larger than the Office.

No exact stage count, pacing, influence effect, resource recipe, or final Atlas visual design is locked by this document.
