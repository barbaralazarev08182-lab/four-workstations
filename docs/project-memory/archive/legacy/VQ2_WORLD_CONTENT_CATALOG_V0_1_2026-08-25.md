# VQ2 World Content Catalog v0.1

> Date: 2026-08-25  
> Status: RESEARCH / PROPOSED — CONTENT DISCOVERY, NOT IMPLEMENTATION AUTHORIZATION  
> Scope: world content families, recovered things, anomalies, persistent changes, traces, Atlas phenomena, relationship density  
> Parent principle: `LOW VERB / HIGH DISCOVERY` — keep controls simple; put complexity into what exists, how it changes, and what it means.

---

# 0. Why this catalog exists

The project should not search for depth by continually adding new input schemes, currencies, minigames, or menus.

The preferred direction is:

> **A small number of understandable actions encounter a large number of surprising world states, objects, relationships, and discoveries.**

The player may repeatedly do familiar actions such as:

- retrieve;
- inspect;
- place;
- keep;
- research;
- cultivate;
- build;
- repair;
- observe.

Freshness should come mainly from:

- **what appears**;
- **what changes**;
- **what reacts**;
- **what connects to something previously seen**;
- **what leaves a trace**;
- **what the player later realizes an old thing actually meant**.

This document therefore asks:

> **What should exist in this world?**

It deliberately does not specify detailed controls, QTE structure, exact drop rates, currencies, UI, or implementation.

---

# 1. Content selection rule — Relationship Density

A world-content idea becomes more valuable when it can participate in several existing systems instead of existing as a one-use collectible.

Working evaluation dimensions:

1. **Immediate curiosity** — would the player want to inspect it before knowing the reward?
2. **Relationship density** — can it meaningfully connect to several systems?
3. **State depth** — can it change over time or under different conditions?
4. **Persistent value** — can it remain relevant after first discovery?
5. **Visual readability** — can the player notice it from the fixed Office camera?
6. **Production leverage** — can one asset/state family create several situations?
7. **Worldbuilding value** — does it teach or complicate the player's understanding of the world?

Useful relation tags in this catalog:

- `[S]` Salvage / retrieval
- `[R]` Research / inspection
- `[C]` Cultivation / growth
- `[B]` Build / repair
- `[A]` Atlas / room-link mystery
- `[E]` Event / anomaly
- `[D]` Display / memory / decoration
- `[X]` Cross-room or later influence potential

The strongest content tends to carry **three or more** tags.

# 2. Recovered Things — first broad candidate pool

These are not all intended for V1. They are a creative pool.

The important design rule is:

> A recovered object should not automatically collapse into `rarity + sell price`.

Its first value is often **uncertainty**.

## 2.1 Familiar-but-wrong objects

These should feel close enough to ordinary life that their wrongness is easy to notice.

### 01. The Returning Piece `[S][R][A][D]`

A smooth ceramic-like fragment. If rotated and left alone, it very slowly turns back toward the same direction.

Possible significance:

- early proof that “direction” outside the room is not ordinary;
- later may align with Atlas orientation;
- visually simple but high mystery value.

### 02. The Heavy Hollow `[S][R][B][D]`

A light-looking hollow shell that behaves as if it is far heavier than its size suggests.

Possible relationships:

- unusual construction ballast;
- salvage equipment strain;
- clue that recovered objects do not fully adopt room physics.

### 03. Dry Water `[S][R][C][E]`

A transparent bead or lump that looks fluid but does not wet anything and keeps a soft shape.

Possible relationships:

- cultivation medium;
- research sample;
- changes near certain anomalies.

### 04. The Warm Nail `[S][R][B][A]`

A blunt metal pin that remains slightly warm without an obvious heat source.

Possible relationships:

- fits certain old mounting points;
- reacts near Atlas components;
- useful in construction, but sacrificing it means losing a clue.

### 05. False Wood `[S][R][B][D]`

A fragment that visually resembles the Office floor material but has the wrong grain continuity and impossible internal layers.

Possible significance:

- supports the “Fracture” hypothesis without proving it;
- could become a visible repair patch.

### 06. The Quiet Bell `[S][R][A][D]`

A bell-shaped object that makes no sound when struck, but occasionally produces a faint tone when Atlas or the room boundary changes.

### 07. The Folded Cup `[S][R][E][D]`

Looks like an ordinary cup compressed through itself in a way that cannot be manufactured normally.

It may remain purely strange, or later reveal a repeated geometric motif.

### 08. The Wrong Shadow Object `[S][R][E][A]`

A simple solid object whose shadow angle is subtly inconsistent with the room's lighting under specific conditions.

## 2.2 Structural / mechanical fragments

These should support building without turning the game into generic wood/stone/metal crafting.

### 09. Indexed Joint `[S][B][A]`

A small connector with several positions, one of which matches a future Atlas or expansion interface perfectly.

### 10. Soft Bearing `[S][R][B]`

A bearing-like ring made from material that bends under pressure but returns precisely to shape.

### 11. Endless Fastener `[S][R][B][A]`

A screw/peg that can be turned for far longer than its visible thread should permit.

### 12. Split Hinge `[S][B][E]`

A hinge whose two leaves seem spatially offset until mounted to the correct pair of surfaces.

### 13. Memory Washer `[S][R][B][D]`

A thin ring that preserves visible wear patterns from the last structure it belonged to.

### 14. Boundary Clamp `[S][B][E][X]`

A clamp that behaves normally inside the room but becomes unusually rigid when mounted near an exposed edge.

### 15. Silent Gear `[S][B][A]`

A gear that refuses to mesh with ordinary mechanisms but fits one Atlas-stage component.

### 16. Soft Anchor `[S][B][E]`

A construction anchor whose material hardens only after being installed into a stabilized edge zone.

---

## 2.3 Living / ambiguous recovered things

These should blur the boundary between item, organism, and structure.

### 17. Closed Seed `[S][R][C][D]`

Looks seed-like but shows no obvious biological signs at first.

Possible branches:

- remain inert;
- open under a specific room condition;
- respond to another recovered object.

### 18. Sleeping Cluster `[S][R][C][E]`

A soft cluster of identical nodules that slowly reorganize when nobody is near.

### 19. Porcelain Moss `[S][R][C][B][D]`

A brittle-looking growth that spreads like moss but hardens like ceramic.

Could become:

- decorative surface;
- repair material;
- anomaly if uncontrolled.

### 20. Breathing Stone `[S][R][C][D]`

A pebble-like object that expands and contracts extremely slowly.

### 21. Thread Nest `[S][R][C][B]`

A ball of fine self-arranging fibers.

Potentially useful for:

- flexible construction;
- cultivation support;
- containment.

### 22. Mirror Spore `[S][R][C][A]`

A small growth that changes form when placed near Atlas-active materials.

### 23. Rootless Sprout `[S][R][C][E][D]`

A sprout-like object that grows while suspended or sitting on bare material rather than soil.

### 24. Listening Shell `[S][R][C][A][D]`

A small shell-like organism/object that opens slightly during certain Atlas signals or room events.

---

## 2.4 Reactive relationship objects

These are especially valuable because they create new discoveries using existing assets.

### 25. Paired Fragments `[S][R][A][D]`

Two visually unrelated pieces react only when both are present in the same room.

Possible behavior:

- align;
- pulse;
- warm;
- point apart/toward each other.

### 26. The Avoider `[S][R][E][D]`

A small object that slowly moves away from one specific material or object family.

### 27. The Follower `[S][R][A][E]`

A small weight or bead that rolls toward Atlas only during certain states.

### 28. Borrowed Color `[S][R][C][D]`

A pale object that gradually adopts the dominant color/material tint of whatever it sits beside.

### 29. Contact Bloom `[S][R][C][E]`

Remains inert until placed on a repaired or previously damaged surface, where it opens or changes.

### 30. Repeater `[S][R][A][E]`

Repeats a vibration/sound pattern it encountered earlier, sometimes much later.

### 31. Temperature Twin `[S][R][E][X]`

One of a possible pair of objects whose temperature or surface response changes when its counterpart is affected elsewhere.

This is late-game compatible but should not imply an approved cross-room mechanic yet.

### 32. Boundary Sleeper `[S][R][E][D]`

Looks completely inert in the room center but changes posture/form near the open edge.

---

## 2.5 Atlas-adjacent clues

These should never all be obvious “Atlas parts.” Some should only become meaningful later.

### 33. Directionless Needle `[S][R][A]`

A needle mounted in a ring that points somewhere inconsistent with room geometry.

### 34. Three-Beat Plate `[S][R][A][B]`

A plate that produces the same three-beat response near specific Atlas phases.

### 35. Miniature Floor Fragment `[S][R][A][D]`

A tiny slab whose proportions resemble a room floor section but do not match the player's room.

### 36. Partial Window Frame `[S][R][A][D]`

A tiny structural frame that looks like part of architecture built at the wrong scale.

### 37. Blank Map Surface `[S][R][A]`

A plaque with no markings until a specific Atlas event causes faint geometry to appear.

### 38. Reversed Fastener `[S][R][A][B]`

Cannot be installed normally, but fits a later Atlas assembly from the “wrong” side.

### 39. Observer Mark `[S][R][A][X][D]`

A mark-bearing object that later resembles the visual language of “being observed,” but appears before the player understands that system.

### 40. Ring Segment `[S][R][A][B][D]`

A curved object that looks decorative at first. Much later, several clues make its circular / chain significance ambiguous but suggestive.

Important guardrail:

> Do not assemble literal physical Ouroboros puzzle pieces that spoil the topology too early.

# 3. Room Anomalies — things that can happen without adding a new minigame

These should use the room itself as the event surface.

## 3.1 Boundary / material anomalies

### A01. Hairline Direction Crack `[E][R][B][A]`

A wall crack forms in an unusual geometry, not following gravity or structural stress.

### A02. Soft Floor Patch `[E][R][B]`

A tiny floor region temporarily compresses more than the surrounding material.

### A03. Embedded Object `[E][R][S]`

A small unknown object appears partially inside a wall/floor material rather than sitting on its surface.

### A04. Edge Frost / Bloom `[E][R][C]`

A temporary growth/pattern appears along the exposed floor edge.

### A05. Local Material Reversal `[E][R]`

A repaired or ordinary surface briefly appears to have its layers reversed or inside-out.

---

## 3.2 Object-behavior anomalies

### A06. One Object Floats Slightly `[E][R][D]`

Not the whole room. One familiar object simply no longer sits correctly.

### A07. Synchronized Turning `[E][R][A]`

Several unrelated recovered objects rotate toward the same unseen direction.

### A08. Delayed Drop `[E][R]`

An object released near the edge pauses or falls in an unexpected delayed way.

### A09. Repeated Sound `[E][R][A]`

A small room sound repeats later from a different location without obvious source.

### A10. Misplaced Return `[E][R][D]`

A persistent small object is found in a nearby but impossible location after a known event.

This should be rare; persistence trust must not be violated casually.

---

## 3.3 Environmental anomalies

### A11. Quiet Light Shift `[E][A]`

A local area changes brightness/color slightly without any lamp or time-of-day cause.

### A12. Dust/Particles Flow Sideways `[E][R][A]`

Fine particles briefly move toward a boundary or Atlas rather than according to room air/gravity.

### A13. Unknown Reflection `[E][R][A]`

A matte/glossy surface briefly suggests geometry that is not in the room.

Use carefully; avoid horror-jump-scare framing.

### A14. The Room Hums `[E][A]`

A low, calm tonal event with no visible source, later associated with certain Atlas or Outside states.

### A15. Edge Depth Shift `[E][R]`

The visual depth beyond an open side feels subtly wrong for a short period — objects/particles appear to move with inconsistent scale or distance cues.

### A16. Familiar Shadow Missing `[E][R]`

A known object temporarily lacks or misplaces its shadow while the rest of the room remains normal.

# 4. Long-change / growth candidates

These are objects or states that give the player a reason to notice tomorrow what they placed today.

### G01. Slow Opening Seed `[C][R][D]`

Changes through several readable stages over days.

### G02. Ceramic Moss Patch `[C][B][D][E]`

Can be cultivated into a decorative/structural patch or become uncontrolled.

### G03. Responsive Crystal `[C][R][A][D]`

Grows different geometry depending on repeated room/Atlas conditions rather than simple fertilizer tiers.

### G04. Thread Colony `[C][R][B][D]`

Self-organizing fibers gradually form a nest, mat, bridge-like mesh, or useful bundle.

### G05. Quiet Companion Object `[C][R][D]`

A barely-living item develops small habitual movements rather than becoming a pet-system with hunger meters.

### G06. Boundary Adapted Growth `[C][R][E]`

Looks ordinary in the room center but develops a distinct form when kept near the open edge.

### G07. Repair-Grown Material `[C][B][D]`

A strange growth used to heal a crack remains visible as a unique patch instead of restoring the wall to factory-new state.

### G08. Signal Bloom `[C][A][D]`

A growth opens only when Atlas receives certain signals.

### G09. Inert-to-Useful Fragment `[R][B][A]`

A piece initially believed decorative is later discovered to fit construction after a knowledge milestone.

### G10. Aging Artifact `[R][D][E]`

Surface markings slowly reveal rather than deteriorate.

### G11. Room-Adapted Object `[R][D][E]`

An unstable salvage item becomes more physically “normal” the longer it stays connected to the room.

### G12. Cross-State Specimen `[C][A][X][D]`

Late-game candidate whose state responds to room-link activity without becoming a direct messaging device.

# 5. Persistent traces — the room should remember what happened

A persistent room should accumulate history rather than repeatedly reset to pristine prefab state.

### T01. Wall Repair Patch

A visible but attractive patch where a prior anomaly was resolved.

### T02. Edge Wear Mark

Subtle abrasion / polish caused by repeated salvage use.

### T03. Replaced Atlas Part Display

An obsolete early Atlas piece remains stored/displayed rather than deleted from history.

### T04. Failed Experiment Stain

A restrained stain/residue pattern that can be cleaned, incorporated, or preserved depending on future design.

### T05. Hand-built Reinforcement

A visible brace or join added after an incident.

### T06. Growth Scar / Fossil

A removed cultivation/anomaly leaves a small shape/material trace.

### T07. First Salvage Trophy

The room preserves one meaningful early recovered object as memory, not necessarily through UI achievement framing.

### T08. Expansion Seam

When the room grows outward, the original floor boundary remains visually legible as part of architectural history.

### T09. Shared Contribution Mark

A construction completed through several real users can retain subtle evidence of multiple contributors without turning into a leaderboard.

### T10. Cross-room Trace `[X]`

Late-game influence may leave a small persistent mark/opportunity that records “something from another node happened here,” while preserving anonymity.

# 6. Atlas phenomena — discovery content, not button unlocks

Atlas should generate **phenomena** before it generates explicit feature labels.

### P01. Uneven Warmth

One Atlas component becomes warm under a repeatable condition.

### P02. Directional Response

A ring/needle/body repeatedly turns toward a direction not explained by the room.

### P03. Three-beat Signal

A recognizable repeating response appears before the player knows it is information.

### P04. Material Echo

Certain recovered objects react to Atlas operation.

### P05. Spatial Smear

Brief incomplete geometry appears above/inside Atlas — too little to identify.

### P06. Repeated Layout Fragment

The same tiny shape/plane appears across several activations.

### P07. Foreign Furniture Recognition

The player first recognizes one piece of furniture/architecture that does not belong to their room.

### P08. Moving Resident

A tiny living figure proves the image/state belongs to another inhabited space.

### P09. Persistent Neighbor Confirmation

Repeated observations show that the same room keeps returning.

### P10. Incoming Observation Reaction

Atlas behaves differently when the player's room is the target of observation.

### P11. Failed Influence Test

Attempting stronger coupling consumes/changes something locally but barely affects the remote state — establishing conservation through experience.

### P12. Ring Evidence

Late accumulated evidence suggests closed-chain topology without presenting a conventional global-network map.

# 7. High Relationship-Density shortlist

From the broad pool above, the following ideas currently have unusually high leverage because they can participate in several systems while remaining mechanically simple.

## Tier A — strongest early/mid candidates

### 1. The Returning Piece

Why strong:

- visually simple;
- immediately strange;
- place/rotate/observe is enough;
- can later connect to Atlas directionality;
- can remain on the desk for weeks before gaining new meaning.

Relationship chain:

`salvage -> inspect -> display -> notice behavior -> compare with Atlas -> reinterpret old discovery`

### 2. Porcelain Moss

Why strong:

- salvage + cultivation + repair + decoration + anomaly potential;
- one visual family can create many states;
- directly turns world history into visible material.

### 3. Listening Shell

Why strong:

- alive/object ambiguity;
- reacts to Atlas without being an obvious machine component;
- supports long-term discovery;
- can be kept/displayed.

### 4. Paired Fragments

Why strong:

- one object can be boring until second discovery;
- encourages memory and comparison rather than new control mechanics;
- can create delayed “I remember that thing” moments.

### 5. Room-Adapted Object

Why strong:

- directly expresses the Outside/world-rule concept;
- visual change over time;
- makes persistence itself part of fiction.

### 6. Hairline Direction Crack

Why strong:

- uses existing wall as content surface;
- can lead to research/repair/trace;
- communicates that anomalies are local and physically persistent.

### 7. Repair-Grown Material

Why strong:

- turns a failure/problem into a lasting beautiful change;
- connects cultivation, building and world memory.

### 8. Directional Atlas + Returning Piece interaction

Why strong:

- two separately understandable systems suddenly connect;
- creates knowledge progression without a new menu.

## Tier B — strong later candidates

### 9. Observer Mark

A clue introduced before the player understands being observed.

### 10. Cross-State Specimen

A living/ambiguous object that reacts to room-link activity.

### 11. Temperature Twin

Potential cross-room relationship object; requires careful fiction/system validation.

### 12. Expansion Seam

# 8. What should NOT become the main content architecture

Avoid filling the catalog primarily with:

- ten tiers of generic ore;
- dozens of near-identical crafting resources;
- standard rarity-colored loot with no behavioral difference;
- collectibles whose only purpose is a collection percentage;
- random events that exist only as reward popups;
- plants that differ only by growth timer/yield;
- furniture whose only function is decoration + price;
- anomalies that are always “clean this away”;
- objects that become useless immediately after their associated quest;
- lore notes that explain mysteries which could have been shown physically.

The desired question is not:

> “How many items are there?”

It is:

> **“How many interesting relationships can emerge from the things that exist?”**

---

# 9. Complexity budget

The project should deliberately preserve mechanical simplicity.

Recommended principle:

> **One new content family should usually reuse an existing verb before it earns a new verb.**

Examples:

Good:

- new object creates a new reason to `inspect`;
- new growth creates a new outcome for `cultivate`;
- new anomaly creates a new choice after `respond`;
- new Atlas signal creates a new interpretation of `observe`.

Use caution:

- new object requires a standalone 40-second minigame;
- every anomaly introduces a separate UI panel;
- every resource family creates another wallet;
- every research object needs a unique control scheme.

A new verb is justified when it opens a broad reusable content family, not when one piece of content needs bespoke interaction.

# 10. Recommended first content constellation — not implementation order

Rather than choosing 30 unrelated items, the strongest early world could revolve around a small **interconnected constellation**.

Possible first constellation:

1. **Returning Piece** — first object with subtle wrong behavior.
2. **Closed Seed** — first long-change object.
3. **Porcelain Moss** — first object that can become both useful and problematic.
4. **Indexed Joint** — first obviously useful construction fragment.
5. **Listening Shell** — first object that later reacts to Atlas.
6. **Hairline Direction Crack** — first room anomaly.
7. **Wall Repair Patch** — first permanent room-memory trace.
8. **Three-beat Atlas Signal** — first repeated phenomenon.
9. **Paired Fragments** — first delayed relationship discovery.
10. **Miniature Floor Fragment** — first clue that a different room-like space may exist.

Why this set is strong:

- only a few interaction verbs are needed;
- every item can be understood visually;
- several things gain new meaning later;
- the room changes physically;
- Atlas enters through evidence rather than exposition;
- not everything is valuable because it is “rare”;
- the player can keep objects and build personal memory around them.

This is a better foundation than starting with a large loot table.

# 11. Design target

The long-term content feeling should be:

> **The player quickly understands what they can do, but remains unsure what the world can do.**

Controls become familiar early.

The surprise budget moves into:

- object behavior;
- delayed relationships;
- persistent changes;
- room history;
- Atlas discoveries;
- another real room's independent life.

This gives the project novelty without turning it into a complicated simulation with dozens of separate systems.

No implementation is authorized by this document.
