# Four Workstations / VQ2 — First-Week Discovery Arc v0.1

> Date: 2026-08-26  
> Status: PRODUCT EXPERIENCE PROPOSAL — DISCUSSION IN PROGRESS  
> Scope: how the first 5–7 meaningful sessions should deepen the player's understanding without materially increasing control complexity  
> Parent: `VQ2_FIRST_HOUR_EXPERIENCE_SEEDS_V0_1_2026-08-26.md`  
> Implementation authorization: NONE

---

# 0. Core conclusion

The first week should not behave like seven tutorial days.

It should behave like seven increasingly meaningful encounters with the **same room**.

The desired progression is:

`I like this place`

-> `the edge gives things back`

-> `some things do not obey the room`

-> `where / how I leave things matters`

-> `the room itself can change`

-> `old discoveries begin to connect`

-> `Atlas is clearly responding to something structured`

The control vocabulary should remain almost unchanged.

The player's **mental model** should change much more than the input model.

Working principle:

> **Week 1 = repeated verbs, changing meaning.**

---

# 1. “Day 1–7” means exposure order, not calendar lock

The labels below are pacing shorthand only.

Do not make the design depend on the player logging in seven consecutive real days.

Preferred rules:

- absence does not create debt or punishment;
- missed events are not permanently lost;
- major discovery beats are gated by prior understanding / actions, not FOMO;
- long-change objects may continue changing while away, but should not skip the player past their meaningful states;
- a slow player can experience this arc over two weeks;
- an active player may compress parts of it, but should not be able to trigger every major revelation in one short binge without enough baseline familiarity.

The purpose is **sequence**, not calendar enforcement.

---

# 2. Complexity budget for the first week

The first week should remain deliberately narrow.

## Reused verbs

Primary actions should still mostly be:

- look / inspect;
- retrieve;
- place / keep;
- Focus / Commit;
- contribute / install;
- later repair;
- observe.

Do not introduce one bespoke minigame per day.

## New-rule budget

Recommended maximum:

- at most one major new world idea per meaningful session;
- at most one new high-relationship object family at a time;
- at most one visually important room-state change at a time.

If three different strange events happen in the same visit, they compete rather than multiply.

## Ordinary-to-strange balance

The first week needs ordinary content for contrast.

Working qualitative ratio:

- majority: understandable / useful / decorative recovered things;
- minority: mildly strange;
- rare: clearly rule-breaking or world-revelatory.

The player should not begin assuming that every object is a puzzle clue.

# 3. Day 1 / Session 1 — Home, Edge, First Return

## Main emotional job

> **“I want to come back here.”**

The first hour follows the existing `3 proofs + 2 promises` structure.

The player experiences:

- a warm inhabited miniature room;
- unusual resident(s);
- open-edge sitting as ordinary behavior;
- first basic retrieval;
- at least one ordinary-ish physical recovered object;
- later a subtle strange object such as the `Returning Piece`;
- one real Focus / Commit producing a visible Atlas construction step;
- one restrained Atlas response.

## What should remain unresolved

- what the Outside is;
- why the Returning Piece moves;
- what Atlas is for;
- whether Atlas reacted because of the installed component, the room, or something else.

## Return hook

The player leaves one or more physical things in the room.

Tomorrow's first reward is not a claimable chest.

It is:

> **the possibility that something they already saw is no longer exactly the same.**

---

# 4. Day 2 / Session 2 — Persistence Becomes Observable

## Main emotional job

> **“The room did not reset while I was gone.”**

Nothing spectacular is required.

The strongest early return experience may simply be:

- the previously recovered object is still where the player left it;
- the installed Atlas piece is still present;
- the `Returning Piece` has returned toward its preferred orientation;
- residents acknowledge / use yesterday's changed room naturally.

This session proves persistence through the environment rather than a save icon.

## Optional small new content

A second retrieval may produce something with delayed potential, such as a `Closed Seed`.

At first it is almost disappointing:

- inert;
- compact;
- no obvious recipe;
- no immediate reward.

That is useful.

The player begins learning:

> **not every good find resolves immediately.**

## Do not do yet

- no wall anomaly;
- no major Atlas escalation;
- no giant reward bundle;
- no “daily task complete” dependency.

Day 2 should make continuity itself feel valuable.

---

# 5. Day 3 / Session 3 — Placement Starts to Matter

## Main emotional job

> **“Where something lives can matter.”**

The game can now demonstrate one mild expression of `Boundary Gradient`, `Stability`, or `Contact Imprinting` without naming those rules.

Possible manifestations:

- the Returning Piece turns more strongly near the exposed edge;
- the Closed Seed changes posture slightly depending on where it is kept;
- a newly retrieved `Porcelain Moss` behaves differently on a plain tray versus near a repaired/edge-like material later;
- a foreign object becomes more stable after remaining in the room.

Only one of these needs to be obvious.

The design goal is not to teach a placement optimization system.

It is to plant an intuition:

> “Putting a strange thing somewhere is not only decoration.”

## Focus relationship

A Focus session can continue a visible Atlas build step or another room project.

Do not make every Focus session unlock a brand-new system.

Repeated Focus should sometimes deepen an existing thing rather than constantly branching the feature tree.

## Return hook

The player should now have at least one object whose future state they genuinely want to check.

This is stronger than a daily streak reminder.

---

# 6. Day 4 / Session 4 — The Room Itself Becomes Content

## Main emotional job

> **“It is not only the objects that can be strange.”**

This is the ideal window for the first restrained room anomaly.

Strong current candidate:

### `Hairline Direction Crack`

The important condition is that the player has already seen this wall intact for several sessions.

Therefore the crack is experienced as a **change**, not initial environment dressing.

Recommended tone:

- small;
- visually readable;
- not catastrophic;
- direction subtly wrong;
- residents notice it;
- player can inspect it;
- no emergency countdown.

The player should have time to leave it alone briefly.

This matters because the world should allow curiosity before cleanup.

## First meaningful decision seed

The player does not need a complicated repair UI.

But they can begin facing an actual choice:

- deal with it safely now;
- leave it for observation;
- test an existing material against it;
- wait for more information.

This is where `Low Verb` must avoid becoming `Low Agency`.

The action remains simple; the **choice of what the room becomes next** matters.

---

# 7. Day 5 / Session 5 — Old Things Start Talking to Each Other

## Main emotional job

> **“These may not be separate weird incidents.”**

This is the first real relationship-density payoff.

Do not necessarily introduce a new item.

Instead use two things the player already knows.

Strong possible chain:

- Atlas produces a repeated response;
- the Returning Piece subtly aligns with it;
- or a `Listening Shell` opens at the same time;
- or the crack's direction resembles the orientation previously seen in the Returning Piece.

The player should be able to form the thought:

> **“Wait — I have seen that pattern before.”**

This is a major product proof.

It demonstrates that the game's content is not a pile of isolated curiosities.

## Three-beat signal timing

The `Three-Beat Atlas Signal` can begin here or slightly later.

If used now, keep it restrained.

Do not decode it.

Do not label it a message.

The value is repeatability.

The player's question becomes:

> “Why does the same thing keep happening?”

---

# 8. Day 6 / Session 6 — Resolve Something, Leave Something Behind

## Main emotional job

> **“Fixing the room does not erase its history.”**

This is the strongest time to complete the first full persistent-history chain.

Recommended chain:

`Hairline Direction Crack`

-> observe / investigate

-> discover or choose a compatible repair approach

-> use `Porcelain Moss` or another authored strange repair material

-> crack stabilizes

-> repaired surface remains visibly different

-> `Wall Repair Patch` becomes part of the room

The key is that success should not mean:

`broken wall -> original prefab wall`

It should mean:

`intact wall -> strange incident -> player response -> new wall history`

This is the first complete proof of:

> **Before -> After -> History**

## Important secondary effect

The repair patch should be allowed to become future content.

For example, much later:

- another growth prefers repaired surfaces;
- a recovered object reacts differently near the patch;
- the patch subtly responds to an Atlas state.

Do not necessarily trigger that during Week 1.

The important idea is that the history object remains **open to future relationships**.

---

# 9. Day 7 / Session 7 — Randomness Becomes Pattern

## Main emotional job

> **“Atlas is not malfunctioning. It is responding to something.”**

Week 1 should end with a stronger question, not a solved mystery.

By now the player may have experienced:

- one or more Atlas construction changes;
- an initial spontaneous Atlas response;
- Returning Piece alignment;
- a repeated sound / motion;
- one living or ambiguous object reacting;
- a directional room anomaly;
- the first permanent repair trace.

The Week-1 climax is simply that one Atlas behavior becomes **clearly repeatable and structured**.

Possible expression:

- the same three-beat cadence returns under comparable conditions;
- the same impossible orientation reappears;
- several known objects respond at once;
- a faint incomplete geometry / plane appears for a moment, but is not yet legible as another room.

The strongest endpoint is not:

> `STRANGER ROOM UNLOCKED`

It is:

> **“Something on the other side of these rules is consistent.”**

This sets up Week 2 / later progression toward spatial echo.

---

# 10. What the player should know after Week 1

The player should intuitively understand most of the following:

1. This room persists.
2. The open edge is normal to inhabit but connects to something abnormal.
3. Retrieved objects can remain in the room and matter later.
4. Some foreign things behave differently depending on location / time / relation.
5. The room itself can develop anomalies.
6. Problems can be handled without simply resetting the room to pristine state.
7. Old objects can become relevant because of later discoveries.
8. Focus / real-life effort helps physically change the world.
9. Atlas is an ongoing construction, not a decorative machine.
10. Atlas's strange behavior is beginning to look structured rather than random.

The player should **not** yet need to know:

- the true nature of the Outside;
- the room-node terminology;
- the global Ouroboros structure;
- who built Atlas;
- what the final Atlas capability is;
- whether another real room is involved;
- the final purpose of recovered fragments;
- the world's origin.

---

# 11. What actually varies across the seven sessions

The first week should avoid repetition by changing the **source of novelty**, not the control scheme.

| Session | Main novelty source |
|---|---|
| 1 | New place + first active boundary interaction |
| 2 | Persistence / delayed object state |
| 3 | Spatial context / placement matters |
| 4 | The room itself changes |
| 5 | Relationship between old phenomena |
| 6 | Player decision creates permanent history |
| 7 | Repetition becomes structured pattern |

This is the target rhythm:

> **same room, same few verbs, different reason to care.**

---

# 12. Solo and shared-room behavior

The arc must be fully completable solo.

With multiple real room members, additional players should enrich the same arc rather than introduce mandatory parallel quests.

Optional shared enrichments:

- another member's Focus contribution visibly completes part of the same Atlas stage;
- one player leaves a recovered object another later notices has changed;
- one member chooses to preserve an anomaly while another later repairs it;
- personal desks make authorship of small discoveries legible;
- room history records contributions through physical traces rather than leaderboard ranking.

Do not require:

- all four members online;
- four unique keys;
- synchronous repair;
- party votes for ordinary progression;
- productivity comparison.

---

# 13. Anti-patterns for Week 1

Do not use the first week to introduce:

- a large store catalog;
- multiple currencies in visible competition;
- deep crafting recipes;
- conventional farming optimization;
- more than one major anomaly family;
- full research-tech trees;
- multiple retrieval minigames;
- large expansion projects;
- stranger-room observation;
- full Ouroboros explanation;
- cross-room influence;
- world-origin exposition;
- heavy daily quest pressure;
- punishment for missing a day.

The first week should build **trust in the world**, not prove feature count.

---

# 14. Week-1 success test

At the end of the first-week experience, a good player reaction is not:

> “I unlocked seven mechanics.”

It is closer to:

> “I know how to use this little place now, but I still don't know what it is capable of.”

Even stronger reactions:

- “I want to see whether that thing changes again.”
- “Why did those two objects react at the same time?”
- “I like that the repaired wall stayed repaired instead of resetting.”
- “The Atlas is definitely responding to something.”
- “I think something we found on Day 1 is going to matter later.”

These reactions are more aligned with the product than:

- “I reached Level 7.”
- “I bought the blue sofa.”
- “I completed 14 daily tasks.”

---

# 15. One-line first-week progression

> **The player spends the first week learning that the room is persistent, the Outside is reachable, strange things have context and memory, the room can carry scars, and Atlas's impossible behavior is beginning to form a pattern — all while using almost the same small set of actions they learned on the first day.**
