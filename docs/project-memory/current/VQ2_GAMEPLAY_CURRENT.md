# VQ2 Gameplay — Current Direction

> Status: **ACTIVE**  
> Updated: 2026-09-08  
> Purpose: accepted gameplay identity and first-hour structure. Old economy/task proposals are not automatically current.

## Core loop

`Inhabit -> Retrieve -> Inspect -> Use/Place -> Observe -> Connect/Reinterpret`

The game should feel like a persistent room that gains meaning through repeated observation and physical change, not a checklist layered over a 3D background.

## First-hour product goal

By the end of the first hour, the player should strongly feel:

1. **I want to make this place my home.**
2. **I want to perform Retrieval again.**
3. **I want to understand what exists outside.**

Retention should come from spatial ownership, physical interaction, Retrieval, visible world response, evidence-based mystery and a room that becomes measurably more personal through player action.

Do not substitute early combat, rarity-color loot, large skill trees, task-list spam, many currencies, repeated QTEs, long forced cinematics or encyclopedia-style tutorials for the actual VQ2 fantasy.

## Mandatory first-hour rules

- Give normal first-person control very quickly, roughly within the first minute.
- Avoid long `walk -> listen -> walk -> listen` exposition chains.
- Dialogue should usually happen while the player is moving, inspecting, placing or operating something.
- Do not force the camera toward every story event.
- Deliver a meaningful new response, interaction, visual change, question or piece of agency frequently enough that the first hour never becomes flat.
- Complete a small version of the full core loop before Chapter 1 ends.
- Onboarding is fail-soft: basic tutorial mistakes should be recoverable and should not catastrophically destroy the Refuge.
- The room must look meaningfully different at the end of the hour than it did at arrival.

## Accepted five-phase first-hour structure

Approximate pacing targets, not hard locks:

### 00–08 min — Arrival / freedom / first ownership

- arrive through the left-side transfer entrance;
- regain movement/look control quickly;
- freely inspect the open Refuge, Atlas, drone, supply crate and Retrieval Station;
- perform one simple useful domestic/utility action;
- make one small persistent ownership/placement choice.

The first reward is not XP. It is: **I touched the room, and the room changed.**

### 08–18 min — Power / supplies / first personal contact

- physically explore the initial supply crate rather than opening a giant inventory grid;
- activate/verify local wireless Power Hub behavior;
- create visible room-wide system feedback;
- establish that the drone is connected to a living outside intelligence/person rather than being only tutorial AI;
- keep the player active during dialogue.

Do not explain full alien motives, total survivor count, full Atlas physics or full cosmology here.

### 18–28 min — Physical Atlas mystery

- reveal the existence of a larger invisible stabilized domain through environmental evidence;
- allow a controlled physical test/probe;
- let the player infer that the missing/open walls are not the true boundary;
- identify Atlas only at the level needed to understand that it stabilizes the local 3D world;
- briefly reveal the boundary through refraction/ripple/distortion, then let it disappear again.

Target question: **Where does my world actually end?**

### 28–43 min — First real Retrieval loop

Teach only the minimum necessary verbs for one successful operation:

- deploy/launch Orb;
- guide/steer;
- connect/couple to a readable Outside-native target;
- retrieve/reel back;
- dock/return.

Important presentation:

- Orb and tether are real 3D objects, not remote menu icons;
- Orb first travels inside the safe domain so distance/tether behavior is understandable;
- boundary crossing visibly reveals Atlas locally;
- opening the Refuge causes a restrained but readable room/system cost;
- first target is low-risk, strange, physically coherent and useful without being a generic glowing loot box;
- the return has weight, sound and physical presence;
- something from Outside should end up **lying in the player's home**.

After return, introduce the multi-value object principle safely: use/process one aspect for a small benefit while preserving an anomalous aspect or unresolved question when appropriate.

By roughly minute 43, the player has experienced:

`Inhabit -> Retrieve -> Inspect -> Use/Place -> Observe -> Reinterpret`

### 43–60 min — Calm contrast -> playable distant war -> next hook

- give the player a short decompression period after first Retrieval success;
- begin the distant fleet/war event while the player retains full camera and movement control;
- do not force a cinematic camera;
- let the player choose where to stand and what to watch;
- Outside light/events may briefly affect the room visually without turning the sequence into a scripted movie;
- Atlas / Refuge should react in a way that makes the distant event personally relevant;
- end with a new unresolved signal/object/condition rather than a lore-dump conclusion.

The war is a **playable world event**, not the opening tutorial's replacement for domestic ownership and Retrieval.

## Room-state transformation requirement

At least some end-of-hour changes should be visibly attributable to the player's actions, for example:

- activated local systems;
- personally placed/assembled object;
- a Retrieved object physically present;
- a new comfort/functional improvement;
- visible aftermath of an Atlas/Outside disturbance;
- a new unresolved signal or outside condition.

Persistent spatial change is central to the game's identity.

## Retrieval decision depth

Retrieved objects should not collapse into one universal currency. A catch can support different choices:

- preserve and observe;
- research/scan/process;
- use as matter/components;
- install/place in the Refuge;
- connect to Atlas or another system;
- keep alive/growing if biological;
- sacrifice one function to gain another.

The design value comes from meaningful opportunity cost and later reinterpretation, not from adding many button verbs.

## Task and progression guardrails

Old task/economy proposals contain useful ideas but are not all locked. Current safe principles are:

- tasks should arise from visible world state, player intent, opportunity, incidents and long-term projects more than from a static Daily Quest list;
- risk should follow voluntary ambition/use, not punish the player for being offline;
- major upgrades should often unlock a new action, target, capability or world layer rather than only `+X%` efficiency;
- world state, knowledge, physical objects and permanent room change can be more valuable rewards than currency;
- exact currencies, economy numbers, crafting trees, cross-room social systems and late-game progression remain open until explicitly re-approved.
