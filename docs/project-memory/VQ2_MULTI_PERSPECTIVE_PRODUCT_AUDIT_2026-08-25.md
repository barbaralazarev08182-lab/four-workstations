# Four Workstations / VQ2 — Multi-Perspective Product Audit

> Date: 2026-08-25  
> Status: AUDIT / DECISION REVIEW — CURRENT DESIGN SNAPSHOT  
> Scope: current VQ2 product direction, world design, interaction/content architecture, commercial clarity, player value, community/reviewer risk  
> This document does **not** authorize implementation or overwrite accepted design rules automatically. It identifies what should be protected, proven, reduced, deferred, or reconsidered.

---

# 0. Executive verdict

## Overall judgment

> **The project is now concept-rich but proof-poor.**

The central problem is no longer “Four Workstations does not have enough game.” The current design already contains enough distinctive product DNA to support a strong game:

- a warm miniature persistent room;
- unusual soft-bodied inhabitants;
- two impossible open edges used as everyday space;
- retrieval of strange things from an unknown Outside;
- objects that change, resonate, remember contact, or gain new meaning later;
- visible scars / repairs / room history;
- a physically growing Atlas;
- real-life Focus feeding persistent world growth;
- eventual observation of one fixed anonymous stranger room;
- later bounded costly cross-room influence.

The new risk is the opposite:

> **too many good concepts can dilute the product promise, delay the strongest payoff, increase production scope, and create a game whose design bible is more exciting than its first playable hours.**

### Audit recommendation

- **Prototype / vertical-slice greenlight: STRONG YES.**
- **Full-production greenlight at the current total scope: NOT YET.**
- **Continue inventing new macro systems: NO, freeze for now.**
- **Continue refining content inside existing rule families: YES, selectively.**
- **Publicly promise the full Ouroboros/influence system before it is proven: NO.**

The next strategic job is not to invent more systems. It is to decide what the buyer is actually buying, what the first playable proof must demonstrate, and which current concepts belong to launch versus later expansion.

# 2. What product do we currently have?

The project is **not** best summarized as:

> “a productivity timer with four desks.”

It is also not best summarized as:

> “a decorating game,” “a farming game,” “a mystery adventure,” or “an MMO.”

The strongest current product identity is:

> **A low-pressure persistent miniature world whose growth is partly powered by real-life effort; its inhabitants retrieve, investigate, build, repair and live with strange things until the room gradually reveals that it is one node in a much larger connected reality.**

For market communication, this should be simplified further.

### Recommended market hierarchy

**Sell the world first.**  
**Use Focus as the fuel / differentiator.**  
**Use Atlas and the stranger-room link as the long-term mystery.**

If the store page leads with “Pomodoro / focus productivity,” buyers will compare the product primarily with dedicated focus companions such as Spirit City and ask whether the utility tools are better.

If the store page completely hides real-life Focus, the project's oldest and most ownable behavioral input loses importance.

The correct hierarchy is therefore neither “productivity app first” nor “Focus does not matter.”

Working principle:

> **The buyer should want the world even before they care about the timer. Real-life Focus then becomes the unusual way that world keeps growing.**

# 4. Internal / creator perspective

## What is strongest

### 4.1 The project has finally escaped the “office productivity skin” trap

The World Bible's “domestic impossibility” framing is substantially stronger than literal office simulation. The office remains a familiar visual bridge, while open edges, strange retrieval, living materials, Atlas, persistent scars and room topology allow the game to become its own world.

This solves a major identity problem without discarding the original shared-room foundation.

### 4.2 Low-Verb / High-Discovery is the correct complexity philosophy

The current preference for a small action vocabulary and a deep result/relationship space is coherent with the desired calm tone and small-team production reality.

The strongest design move made recently is **not any single item idea**. It is the shift from:

`more depth -> more minigames`

to:

`more depth -> more states / relationships / reinterpretations using the same verbs`.

### 4.3 “Before -> After -> History” is a real differentiator

Hozy proves that visible transformation is satisfying. Four Workstations' stronger extension is that the room does not merely become prettier; it accumulates evidence that things happened there.

A repaired crack, an old Atlas component, an expansion seam, an edge wear mark, a long-lived object and another player's contribution can create emotional ownership without requiring endless decoration inventory.

### 4.4 The 7 rule families are enough

The rule-family layer is now at a healthy conceptual size:

- Stability / belonging
- Boundary gradient
- Affinity / direction
- Resonance
- Contact imprinting
- Growth
- Conservation + trace

Adding more universal rules now would likely reduce clarity faster than it increases possibility.

### 4.5 The creature redesign is strategically good

The creature direction solves two problems simultaneously:

- stronger product identity;
- lower contact/animation complexity than human rigs.

PEBBLE / SEED-like ambiguous soft inhabitants have high potential, but visual differentiation still needs proof.

## Internal warning

The team should recognize that **research/design output has crossed the sufficiency threshold**.

There is now enough conceptual material to rationalize almost any new feature. That is dangerous.

New ideas should now be rejected unless they strengthen one of the already-existing product promises.

# 5. Potential buyer perspective

A Steam buyer does not see the design bible. They see:

- capsule;
- screenshots;
- first seconds of a trailer;
- short description;
- price;
- review score;
- feature list.

Steamworks explicitly warns that a Discovery Queue viewer may give a game less than roughly ten seconds to make an impression, recommends gameplay first in trailers, and requires screenshots to show actual gameplay. Therefore “it becomes interesting after I explain the ontology of the Outside” is not a marketable answer.

## Likely positive first reaction

If executed well, the visual could create a very strong sequence:

1. “That is a beautiful tiny room.”
2. “What are those little creatures?”
3. “Why are two sides of the room open?”
4. “Why is one creature sitting with its legs over the edge?”
5. “What is the other one pulling up from there?”
6. “What is that thing being built in the center?”

This is much stronger than four humans sitting at four computers.

## Likely buyer questions / friction

A potential buyer will still need fast answers to:

- What do I actually do?
- Is this primarily a game or a productivity tool?
- Do I need three friends?
- Do I need to perform real work before I am allowed to play?
- Is this mostly idle / waiting?
- Is it a decorating sandbox?
- How much of the mysterious stuff actually exists at launch?
- Why can a stranger observe my room?
- Is online connectivity required?

If the store page tries to answer every one equally, it becomes muddy.

## Buyer verdict

**Wishlist potential: high.**  
**Purchase confidence today: not yet proven.**

The visual concept can earn curiosity. The playable loop must earn the sale.

## Brand/name warning

The title **Four Workstations** increasingly describes the historical visual starting point more clearly than the current long-term fantasy.

This is not an instruction to rename the game now, but it should become a future brand audit question:

> Does the title help the buyer imagine the current game, or does it over-anchor expectations around coworkers / office productivity?

# 6. Actual player perspective

## What players are likely to love

### 6.1 A home that becomes personal because of events, not only decoration

A room that retains repairs, growth, old objects and shared construction can create stronger attachment than a room whose identity depends mainly on selecting furniture from a catalogue.

### 6.2 Discovery without control overload

The current object/rule design can support the experience:

> “I already know how to interact, but I do not know what this thing will do.”

That is a strong fit for players who are tired of large crafting trees, inventory micromanagement, farming chores or total free-form decorating.

Selected CozyGamers discussions show a real, if anecdotal, audience that finds unrestricted decorating stressful and prefers structured unlocks / predetermined placement, while other players complain that cozy games become boring when there is no meaningful friction at all.

The current design's combination of **curated placement + optional risk that follows ambition** can potentially satisfy both pressures.

### 6.3 No absence punishment is correct

A persistent companion-world tied to work/study should not greet the user with debt, dead crops, ruined streaks or destroyed structures after a difficult week.

This is both product-aligned and community-friendly.

### 6.4 Delayed reinterpretation is powerful

`Fragment A` becoming meaningful when `Fragment B` appears days later, or an early Returning Piece gaining meaning after Atlas responds, can produce stronger memory than ordinary rarity progression.

This is one of the best current retention ideas.

## What players are likely to dislike if executed poorly

### 6.5 Low Verb can accidentally become Low Agency

Simple controls are not enough.

If the player's actual experience becomes:

`look -> wait -> collect -> wait -> look again`,

then “deep world” will feel like designer language covering passivity.

The game still needs meaningful choices such as:

- keep or use;
- place here or there;
- stabilize or push risk;
- preserve or repair;
- sacrifice an object for a project or keep it as evidence;
- act now or wait for more information.

### 6.6 Real-life Focus can become a gate instead of a meaningful input

This is one of the largest product tensions.

If all interesting play is locked behind mandatory real-life work, game-first buyers may feel that the product is withholding itself.

If Focus produces almost nothing unique, the founding premise becomes decorative.

Audit principle:

> **Focus should fuel and shape progression, not monopolize player agency.**

### 6.7 Waiting can masquerade as discovery

Closed seeds, long growth, delayed signals and persistent changes are attractive only when the player has enough active things to understand / decide / arrange between those moments.

Do not build retention primarily from timers.

### 6.8 Persistent history can become clutter

The exact strength of scars, kept objects, growths and expansion can eventually become a visual weakness in a tiny room.

The world will need some future curation / storage / archive / selective-removal logic even if unrestricted decoration is never used.

### 6.9 Mystery can become obscurity

Subtle clues are good; dependence on a wiki is not.

The player should be able to form hypotheses from readable repeated behavior, not guess which one of forty invisible variables changed.

# 7. Seller / publisher / commercial perspective

## 7.1 The project has a strong marketable visual identity — if it stops looking like a normal office

Adjacent successful Steam products demonstrate that simple core mechanics are not inherently a commercial weakness:

- Tiny Glade currently presents itself as a small diorama builder with no management, combat or goals and carries very strong Steam sentiment.
- Spirit City very clearly sells itself as a gamified focus tool and also carries very strong sentiment.
- Rusty's Retirement clearly sells the “bottom-of-screen idle companion” promise.

The lesson is not “copy one of them.” It is:

> **Simple products can sell when the promise is legible and the experience delivers exactly that promise.**

Four Workstations' problem is currently not too little complexity; it is positioning complexity.

## 7.2 Price anchoring is currently ambiguous

Adjacent Steam prices at the time of this audit include roughly:

- Rusty's Retirement: **$6.99**
- Spirit City: **$11.99**
- Tiny Glade: **$14.99**
- Hozy: **$14.99**

These are not direct comps and should not be used as a pricing formula.

However, they expose a buyer expectation:

- if Four Workstations reads mainly as a productivity companion, buyers may benchmark it against a low-teens utility/cozy product;
- if it reads as a persistent systemic game with mystery, shared rooms and long-term world state, buyers will expect much more launch content / reliability.

A higher-value game position is plausible, but only if the world systems are truly present rather than roadmap promises.

## 7.3 Online persistence creates hidden operating cost

The Ouroboros concept is one of the most ownable product ideas and one of the largest commercial liabilities.

A seller must eventually solve:

- account / room persistence;
- inactive neighboring rooms;
- deleted accounts;
- chain maintenance;
- offline-room representation;
- server / database cost;
- abuse / moderation;
- privacy / consent expectations;
- long-term service support after the initial sale.

A one-time premium purchase with permanent online service has a real lifetime-cost model. This must be understood before the stranger-chain feature becomes a commercial promise.

## 7.4 The full ring should not be marketed before it is launch-ready

Steam's store-review guidance requires store pages to represent features/content that will be available at launch, or clearly label unreleased future features.

Therefore the strongest lore idea can become a marketing trap if the launch version only contains the office and Focus timer.

Do not sell “persistent anonymous stranger worlds” if buyers cannot actually experience that in the version being sold.

## 7.5 Monetization fit

Current product values fit best with low-pressure monetization:

- premium base product;
- potentially later substantial cosmetic / world-content / expansion DLC;
- no pay-to-progress Atlas;
- no FOMO battle-pass dependency;
- no paid productivity power.

This is not a frozen business model, but aggressive F2P/FOMO structures would conflict with the no-absence-punishment and persistent-home identity.

# 8. Community / reviewer perspective

## Best plausible review narrative

If the game succeeds, the community/press story could sound like:

> “A cozy game that is not another farming sim. You live with strange little creatures in one tiny room that remembers what happened there. Simple actions keep producing odd relationships. The central machine eventually reveals a persistent anonymous neighbor, and the game makes real-world Focus feel like fuel for a living place rather than a score.”

That is a strong review identity.

## Worst plausible review narrative

If execution misses, criticism could sound like:

> “A Pomodoro app wrapped in beautiful lore. You wait for timers, pull random collectibles from a void, and most of the exciting Atlas/social ideas arrive late or are promised for the future. The anonymous observation system is creepy, the currencies feel gamey, and the supposedly systemic objects are mostly scripted one-offs.”

This is also highly plausible unless the product is disciplined.

## 8.1 Community will notice derivative creature design immediately

The soft-body direction is strategically good, but “cute bean/seed/blob creature” is a crowded visual space.

A final character that is only “round body + eyes + sprout” will be compared with existing cute creature games regardless of internal lore originality.

The silhouette/material/motion language needs its own authorship.

## 8.2 “Not another farming/decorating game” is a real opportunity

Community discussions repeatedly show fatigue with:

- mandatory farming;
- huge decoration sandboxes;
- grind-heavy crafting;
- too many chores;
- games so easy that nothing has consequence.

This is anecdotal rather than representative market research, but it supports the project's current restraint:

- growth without conventional farming;
- structured placement rather than unlimited interior design;
- risk by choice rather than punishment;
- knowledge / world consequences instead of endless recipes.

## 8.3 Privacy framing can dominate discussion if mishandled

“An anonymous stranger watches your room” is memorable, but the phrase can easily sound like surveillance rather than wonder.

The current diegetic rule — the target knows observation is active, identity is hidden, state is represented through a miniature rather than a webcam — is a good start.

Still, community trust will depend on clear boundaries and user control.

This feature must never rely on ambiguity about what personal data or live activity is exposed.

# 9. The most important design tensions

These tensions are not mistakes. They are tradeoffs that need explicit decisions.

| Tension | Strength | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus as real-life input** | Unique behavioral hook | Feels like work-gating a game |
| **Long mystery reveal** | Strong emotional payoff | Best selling point arrives too late |
| **One fixed stranger room** | Rare persistent social relationship | Dead/inactive neighbor destroys value |
| **Low verbs** | Accessible, calm, production-efficient | Passive / shallow if choices lack consequence |
| **Seven reusable world rules** | Content leverage | QA/state-combination explosion if over-simulated |
| **Persistent scars/history** | Emotional ownership | Tiny room becomes cluttered/noisy |
| **Soft non-human creatures** | Brand + cheaper animation | Generic “cute blob” comparison |
| **One main room** | Identity and continuity | Buyers may perceive low content quantity |
| **Coins / catalog** | Familiar economy and choice | Pulls product back toward generic shop loop |
| **Atlas as mythic spine** | Strong long-term narrative | Everything becomes “an Atlas clue,” shrinking the world |
| **Anonymous observation** | Distinctive social mystery | Creepiness/privacy/moderation concern |

# 10. What should be LOCKED as product DNA

These are the strongest ideas in the current body of work and should survive scope reduction.

1. **Domestic Impossibility** — warm familiar room + quietly impossible rules.
2. **One persistent room that accumulates history.**
3. **Low Verb / High Discovery.**
4. **One open edge is ordinary life/rest space.**
5. **One open edge reaches into an unknown Outside and brings things back.**
6. **Recovered things are objects with identity/relationships, not only loot values.**
7. **Important world events leave visible traces.**
8. **Non-human inhabitants optimized for readable physical interaction.**
9. **Atlas grows physically and changes the player's understanding of the world.**
10. **Real-life effort produces visible persistent world progress.**
11. **Solo is complete; additional people enrich but do not gate.**
12. **No punishment for absence; risk follows ambition.**
13. **System scaling over endless furniture/content-pack dependence.**
14. **Semantic / curated placement rather than immediate unrestricted 3D freedom.**

These collectively form a coherent product.

# 11. What should be STRENGTHENED before more ideation

## 11.1 A single buyer-facing promise

The project needs one primary sentence that all major features support.

Working candidate:

> **现实中的专注，会让一个温暖而不可能的小世界慢慢活起来。**

A more game-descriptive version:

> **在一个会记住你们经历的微缩房间里生活、打捞未知、建造并发现：这个小世界从来不是孤立的。现实中的专注会推动它继续成长。**

Neither is final marketing copy. The point is hierarchy.

## 11.2 Immediate tactile delight

Only a handful of signature verbs need Hozy-level feel, but those few must be excellent:

- retrieve/pull;
- pick up / inspect / place;
- Atlas install / response;
- build / repair.

## 11.3 Early visible strangeness

The world should establish its “impossible but calm” identity early. Do not make the first hours only an office timer while saving every strange thing for later.

## 11.4 Creature authorship

The final inhabitant needs a silhouette/motion/material signature that can survive comparison screenshots without looking like a generic cute mascot.

## 11.5 Readable room history

Players should understand that a patch / seam / retained part is history, not graphical damage or clutter.

# 12. What should be DE-EMPHASIZED or reconsidered

## 12.1 Coins / shop as a central emotional loop

Coins can remain useful common liquidity, but the newer world design has outgrown `Focus -> Coins -> Shop` as a primary fantasy.

Be willing to shrink the economic role of Coins further if prototypes show that world objects, discoveries and projects provide enough motivation.

## 12.2 Literal office/productivity presentation

The office should remain the starting visual grammar, not the thematic ceiling.

Avoid marketing or art choices that push the product back toward:

- four employees;
- corporate task dashboards;
- KPI-like productivity scores;
- “work harder to buy desk decorations.”

## 12.3 Conventional daily quest / checklist structure

A physical wall board can be useful, but the room should generate situations and opportunities rather than become a second task manager.

## 12.4 Large generic loot/material inventories

The 40-item content catalog is a design pool, not a launch obligation.

The 10 core seeds are already more valuable than a large rarity table.

---

# 13. What should be DEFERRED until the core proves itself

These ideas may remain important, but should not drive near-term scope:

- exact world-origin answer;
- large-scale room expansion topology;
- many expansion facilities;
- full global Ouroboros operations;
- cross-world influence system;
- identity reveal/social progression around the stranger;
- large free-placement sandbox;
- huge catalogue of reactive objects;
- deep economy balancing;
- many Atlas phases;
- conventional crafting trees.

Especially important:

> **The Ouroboros concept should remain protected, but implementation/business promises should wait until persistence, inactive-neighbor handling, privacy boundaries and the local room loop are proven.**

# 15. The single biggest audit conclusion

Four Workstations should no longer ask:

> “What other systems can we add so the game is not too simple?”

It should ask:

> **“Which minimum set of things makes a buyer immediately understand the fantasy, and makes a player come back because the same room keeps producing meaning?”**

The current project has enough ideas.

The next phase should be **hierarchy and proof**.

# 16. Recommended next design work — still about WHAT, not HOW

Before returning to detailed implementation, define three layers.

## A. Product Promise

Freeze:

- who the primary buyer is;
- the one-sentence promise;
- the three buyer-visible pillars;
- what the product is **not**.

## B. Launch/Core Content Boundary

Classify every current concept into:

- **Must exist in the core sold experience**
- **May exist if proof is strong**
- **Post-launch / later-world expansion**
- **Research only / mystery reserve**

This is a content decision, not a mechanics decision.

## C. Experience Evidence Ladder

Define what a player must have **experienced**, not how it is coded:

- very early: “this is not a normal office”;
- early: “the Outside returns things I want to understand”;
- early-mid: “my room remembers what happened”;
- later: “old things can gain new meaning”;
- major progression: “Atlas is showing a real other place”;
- long term: “my room belongs to a larger persistent structure.”

Only after these are frozen should the project decide precise timings, UI, formulas, drop tables, QTEs or economy numbers.

# 17. Audit caveats

- Current ratings/prices of reference games are snapshots and will change.
- Steam-review comparisons are directional market context, not evidence that Four Workstations will achieve similar results.
- Reddit/community discussions are anecdotal and useful for identifying possible attitudes, not estimating market share.
- The project has not yet generated reliable playtest/retention data for the new VQ2 world direction.
- Many VQ2 documents are intentionally `PROPOSED`; this audit judges the combined current direction without pretending every detail is canon.

Final audit state:

> **High-conviction concept. Medium-low proof. Stop broadening; start selecting.**
