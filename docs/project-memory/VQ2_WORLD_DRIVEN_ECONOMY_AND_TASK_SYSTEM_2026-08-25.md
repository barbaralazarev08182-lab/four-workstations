# VQ2 World-Driven Economy & Task System

> Date: 2026-08-25  
> Status: RESEARCH / PROPOSED — DISCUSSION IN PROGRESS  
> Scope: economy, character behaviors, task structure, progression, punishment/consequence design, room/world growth  
> Purpose: replace an overly literal "office productivity + coins + furniture shop" framing with a broader world-driven game system

This document records a major design correction:

> Four Workstations must not behave like a realistic office with a decorative shop attached. The Office is a small strange world/node whose inhabitants can work, rest, explore, salvage, cultivate, repair, research, build, observe, and later affect other linked worlds.

The player should not spend most of the game watching a character sit at a computer.

The economy should not reduce to:

`Focus -> Coins -> Buy Decoration`

That loop can remain one layer, but it cannot be the whole game.

---

# 1. Core design reframe

The current room should be treated as a **habitat / workshop / world-node**, not merely a workplace.

The visual starting point may resemble an office, but the game logic should become progressively less realistic as the world develops.

Existing unusual spatial ideas support this direction:

- two walls + two open floor edges;
- characters can sit at an exposed edge like a cliff;
- one edge may support a fishing-like action into an unknown void;
- the central Atlas is gradually constructed;
- Atlas eventually reveals a fixed stranger room in an Ouroboros-like chain;
- the room may later expand outward with attached platforms;
- wall surfaces may host physical task/information systems;
- walls or room boundaries may later fracture/open/reveal strange spaces or events.

The Office should therefore become a place where normal and impossible activities coexist.

---

# 2. World premise — design hypothesis, not locked lore

A useful design hypothesis is:

> Each Office is a small inhabited node suspended inside a larger unknown structure. Rooms are separated but connected in a global closed chain. The inhabitants maintain, study, expand, and reconnect their node while recovering strange things from outside it.

This is **not yet frozen lore**.

Its purpose is to make systems feel causally related:

- Focus/work can create useful ordered energy/progress.
- Rest can restore a character or room condition.
- Void salvage can retrieve unknown objects/materials.
- Research can identify those objects.
- Construction can turn discoveries into room capabilities.
- Atlas can reconnect one node to another.
- Risky experiments can destabilize the room.
- Repairs and containment become meaningful tasks.

The game should feel like maintaining and growing a tiny impossible world, not decorating a normal office.

# 3. Character behavior library — broader than office work

The future character system should support a small but expressive set of behavior families.

The exact animations are not yet implementation requirements.

## 3.1 Focus / Work behaviors

Real-world Focus remains an important gameplay input, but the in-world representation does not have to be "stare at monitor" every time.

Possible visual behaviors:

- typing / reading;
- drawing blueprints;
- assembling a tiny device;
- sorting strange parts;
- examining a recovered object;
- tuning a small instrument;
- writing on paper / board;
- winding or calibrating a machine;
- tending a desk experiment.

The same real Focus session can therefore produce varied in-world behavior.

## 3.2 Rest / Recovery behaviors

Possible behaviors:

- sit at the open edge with legs hanging over the void;
- lie down on the floor;
- curl up / compress if the future creature design supports it;
- drink / snack;
- watch the Atlas quietly;
- inspect the distant void;
- sleep in an unusual physical location rather than a normal sofa.

Rest should eventually have systemic value rather than existing only as decoration.

## 3.3 Salvage / Retrieval behaviors

Possible behaviors:

- lower a line into the void;
- pull up debris / organisms / objects;
- use a crane, magnet, hook, net, or future strange tool;
- recover something lodged under the room edge;
- retrieve a drifting object that approaches the room.

This is a major candidate for active world gameplay.

## 3.4 Research / Identification behaviors

Recovered things should not always arrive as fully understood rewards.

Possible actions:

- inspect;
- scan;
- open;
- dissect / separate;
- identify;
- classify;
- combine;
- test inside a small device;
- compare against a wall-board/archive record.

This creates a converter loop rather than "loot -> sell" only.

## 3.5 Construction / Repair behaviors

Possible actions:

- assemble Atlas parts;
- repair a damaged machine;
- reinforce a floor edge;
- install a platform module;
- patch a wall crack;
- intentionally widen a strange opening;
- replace a worn component;
- stabilize an anomaly;
- build a small structure on a future expansion platform.

## 3.6 Cultivation behaviors

Growth systems do not need to be normal houseplants.

Possible targets:

- ordinary plants;
- strange seeds recovered from the void;
- fungus;
- crystalline growth;
- tiny moving organisms;
- objects that slowly transform through stages;
- hybrid plant-machine structures.

The important mechanic is **persistent staged growth**.

## 3.7 Atlas / Link behaviors

Possible later behaviors:

- charge Atlas;
- calibrate it;
- align a ring/module;
- observe the fixed stranger room;
- respond to a signal;
- later prepare a costly cross-world influence;
- repair link instability.

## 3.8 Event-response behaviors

The world can occasionally create temporary situations:

- a wall begins cracking;
- gravity briefly shifts;
- objects start floating;
- lights pulse unexpectedly;
- an unknown signal appears;
- void residue accumulates;
- a strange organism escapes its container;
- something heavy catches the salvage line;
- Atlas produces an abnormal reading.

These situations can generate tasks without requiring new maps.

# 4. Task system should be "situations and projects", not only daily checklist quests

A normal productivity app asks:

- Work 25 min.
- Check in.
- Complete 3 tasks.

Four Workstations should additionally ask:

- What happened in the room today?
- What does the room need?
- What strange opportunity appeared?
- What project are we trying to finish?
- What risk are we willing to take?

Recommended task families:

## A. Routine Tasks

Low-risk, predictable.

Examples:

- complete Focus;
- repair a minor object;
- calibrate Atlas;
- tend a growth object;
- sort recovered materials.

Rewards: XP, Coins, small predictable materials/progress.

## B. World Situations

Generated from room state/events.

Examples:

- a crack appears in the wall;
- a device jams;
- an unknown seed begins reacting;
- a strange object is caught below the platform;
- Atlas detects an irregular pulse.

The player should often have more than one response option.

Example:

`Wall Crack`

- Patch it safely -> costs material, restores stability.
- Study it first -> delayed repair, chance of research discovery.
- Intentionally open it -> higher risk, possible rare world event.

This creates choice instead of checklist completion.

## C. Projects

Longer persistent goals.

Examples:

- Atlas phase construction;
- build a salvage rig;
- create a cultivation platform;
- reinforce an expansion edge;
- build a new small external structure;
- restore a damaged world feature.

Projects should visibly transform the room.

## D. Experiments / Risk Contracts

Optional high-risk tasks.

The player intentionally opts in and accepts a cost/failure consequence.

Examples:

- Deep Salvage;
- unstable seed experiment;
- force an Atlas signal decode;
- open a suspicious wall cavity;
- overcharge a device for a rare result.

Success: rare material, blueprint, discovery, special event.

Failure: lost stake, damaged tool, temporary instability, anomaly, reduced reward, or recovery task.

This is the preferred place for meaningful punishment because the player knowingly chose the risk.

## E. Social / Chain Situations

After Atlas observation unlocks:

- detect that another room is observing this room;
- observe a meaningful change in the fixed stranger room;
- later prepare a costly influence toward the linked room;
- later respond to an anonymous influence arriving from elsewhere in the ring.

These should not become conventional friend-chat quests.

---

# 5. Rewards should not collapse into money

The current high-level three-resource structure remains useful:

- **XP** — permanent player growth, not spendable.
- **Coins** — common fungible spending currency.
- **Build Charge** — behavior-bound Atlas/shared-project resource.

Do not automatically add a fourth fungible currency.

However, gameplay may produce **physical items/materials** that are not currencies.

Possible examples:

- salvage scrap;
- strange seeds/spores;
- resonant fragments;
- broken components;
- unknown artifacts;
- organism samples;
- blueprints;
- one-off relics;
- unstable matter.

These are inventory/world objects with specific uses, not interchangeable money balances.

The economy therefore becomes:

`Behavior -> value in several forms -> conversion / construction / risk / world change`

rather than:

`Behavior -> Coins -> Shop`

---

# 6. Why money must never become the universal solution

A late-game economy fails if one resource can solve every problem.

Therefore Coins should be good for **predictable/common** needs:

- common parts;
- ordinary decor;
- basic tools;
- cosmetic commissioning;
- routine repair materials;
- certain upgrades;
- service/conversion fees.

Coins should **not** directly buy:

- Atlas core progress;
- every rare material;
- every discovery;
- every world unlock;
- stranger-world access;
- instant completion of all projects.

Important structures should often require a combination such as:

`Coins + specific recovered object + Build Charge + blueprint/discovery`

or

`material set + completed research + construction progress`

This prevents a player with a huge Coin balance from skipping the actual game.

---

# 7. Anti-inflation principle — activity creates both income and expense

A critical long-term rule:

> The systems that create value should also create reasons to consume value.

Do not rely only on a finite shop catalogue as the Coin sink.

Potential recurring sinks:

- tool wear caused by actual use;
- replacement components;
- lures / hooks / containers / experiment inputs;
- device calibration after heavy use;
- repairs after risky activities;
- crafting/conversion fees;
- new project modules;
- optional high-cost experiments;
- later costly cross-world influence;
- room expansion and higher-capability infrastructure.

Prefer **usage-driven costs** over passive offline taxes.

Example:

- If the player does not use the salvage rig, it does not arbitrarily decay overnight.
- If the player performs many difficult salvages, the line/tool accumulates wear and eventually needs repair.

This scales sinks with active players while avoiding punishment for absence.

External economy-design research strongly supports maintaining meaningful sinks relative to sources to prevent soft-currency inflation.

# 8. Punishment / consequence design

The game should contain consequences, but they should create decisions and stories rather than simply punish the user for having a life outside the game.

## 8.1 No default absence punishment

Avoid as primary design:

- login streak loss;
- permanent decay while offline;
- losing major progress because the player did not open the game;
- mandatory daily chores whose failure destroys owned content.

## 8.2 Failure should matter when the player accepts risk

Best location for stronger punishment:

> Optional contracts / experiments / dangerous world actions.

Examples of consequences:

- lose the item used as bait/input;
- damage a tool;
- receive reduced rewards;
- create a temporary anomaly;
- generate room instability;
- produce residue/mess that needs recovery;
- temporarily disable one facility;
- create a follow-up recovery task;
- lose an opportunity window tied to the chosen experiment.

## 8.3 Consequences should often create new gameplay

Preferred:

`Failed experiment -> strange growth escapes -> contain/research it`

instead of:

`Failed experiment -> -500 Coins -> nothing else happens`

Preferred:

`Deep salvage fails -> line snaps -> wall edge gets damaged -> repair or study debris`

instead of:

`Deep salvage fails -> generic red error message`

The failure state should often be content.

## 8.4 Standard Focus vs Risk Focus

Do not heavily punish an ordinary Focus session that the user cancels because real life interrupted them.

Possible future distinction:

- Normal Focus: incomplete session simply gives reduced/no completion reward.
- Voluntary Contract Focus: player stakes a resource for a higher reward; failure loses the stake or creates a small consequence.

This gives real stakes without making the productivity layer hostile.

# 9. Room condition systems — possible non-currency pressures

Instead of solving all balance problems with more currencies, the world can have conditions/states.

Possible future conditions:

- **Stability** — room/Atlas structural or anomalous condition.
- **Wear** — per-tool/per-device use-based condition.
- **Contamination / Residue** — generated by risky void/experiment activity.
- **Character Fatigue / Need for Rest** — potentially used to make edge-rest behavior meaningful.
- **Facility Load / Capacity** — expansion increases capability but may require infrastructure support.

These are not necessarily global numeric HUD bars. They can often be represented physically in the room.

Examples:

- unstable Atlas flickers or misaligns;
- worn tool visibly bends/sparks;
- contamination appears as strange growth/residue;
- tired creature walks to the edge and sits;
- over-capacity storage visibly fills.

Do not lock these exact conditions yet. They are tools for future design.

# 10. Upgrade philosophy — unlock new verbs, not only bigger numbers

The strongest upgrade should answer:

> "What new thing can I now do?"

rather than only:

> "+15% income."

Recommended upgrade categories:

## Capability upgrades

Examples:

- basic salvage -> deeper salvage;
- inspect object -> analyze object;
- Atlas inactive -> observation;
- observation -> later influence;
- plant shelf -> strange cultivation chamber;
- wall board -> anomaly/archive functions.

## Spatial upgrades

Examples:

- new external platform;
- edge rig;
- small greenhouse/garden structure;
- workshop nook;
- observation structure;
- additional room appendage.

## Processing upgrades

Examples:

- turn raw salvage into components;
- identify unknown items more reliably;
- combine materials;
- safely contain unstable objects.

## Capacity upgrades

Examples:

- more storage;
- more experiment slots;
- more desk/world anchor slots;
- more active projects.

## Expression upgrades

Cosmetics, rare variants, creature appearance, Atlas style, room identity.

Numerical efficiency upgrades may exist, but should not dominate progression.

# 11. Example world loops

## Loop A — Focus to construction

`Real Focus`
-> XP + Coins + limited Build Charge
-> Build Charge invested into Atlas
-> Atlas physically changes
-> later capability unlocks

This preserves the current core thesis.

## Loop B — Void salvage

`Use edge salvage interaction`
-> consume/wear tool/input
-> retrieve unknown object
-> inspect it
-> choose to sell / use / research / cultivate / build
-> possible anomaly/failure

The value is not automatically converted into Coins.

## Loop C — Strange cultivation

`Recover seed/spore/object`
-> choose a cultivation slot
-> growth stages respond to time/Focus/care
-> mature result becomes material, organism, decor, blueprint clue, or new world interaction

## Loop D — Anomaly response

`Room event appears`
-> choose safe or risky response
-> spend material / time / capability
-> room state changes
-> success/failure produces a visible outcome

## Loop E — Cross-world later game

`Build Atlas sufficiently`
-> observe fixed stranger room
-> target knows it is being observed but observer remains anonymous
-> later unlock an influence action
-> influence consumes a meaningful scarce input/cost
-> target world receives a visible effect or opportunity

# 12. Example task situations — deliberately non-office-like

These are exploration examples, not locked content.

### "Something is pulling the line"

A salvage line becomes stuck below the platform.

Choices:

- Cut it now — lose the line/input, avoid danger.
- Pull normally — chance to recover a heavy object.
- Overload the winch — greater reward chance, possible tool damage.

### "The wall is breathing"

A previously solid wall develops a soft moving crack.

Choices:

- Seal it.
- Scan it.
- Open it deliberately.

Possible results: resource, blueprint, temporary hole, anomaly, hidden niche.

### "A seed refuses gravity"

A recovered seed grows downward/upward/sideways in an impossible way.

Choices:

- continue cultivation;
- harvest it early;
- move it to an external platform;
- feed it a rare recovered material.

### "Atlas received a pulse"

The central device produces an unknown signal.

Choices depend on progression:

- record;
- stabilize;
- decode;
- ignore;
- later redirect/influence.

### "Loose objects"

A short gravity disturbance causes small room props to float.

Player/character can secure valuable objects, observe the event, or deliberately expose an experiment to it.

# 13. Spatial consequences for Office Final Polish

Office Final Polish must now preserve room capacity for future systems, not only decoration.

Do not fill every empty area.

Potential reserved functional zones:

- central Atlas and clearance;
- edge-sitting area;
- edge salvage interaction area;
- wall information/archive/anomaly board;
- desk interaction/module slots;
- one or more small research/processing points;
- cultivation locations;
- future external platform connection points;
- room-event staging space;
- possible wall transformation/opening zones.

The scene should retain **negative space for future verbs**.

---

# 14. Current strongest design principles

1. **The Office is a strange world-node, not a literal office simulator.**
2. **Characters need a broad behavior vocabulary beyond computer work.**
3. **Tasks should come from world situations, projects, discoveries and risks — not only checklist productivity quests.**
4. **Rewards must include physical items, discoveries, materials, capabilities and world state changes — not only money.**
5. **Coins must never become a universal answer.**
6. **No fourth fungible currency should be added merely to fix inflation.**
7. **Use item/material specificity and world conditions instead of endless currency proliferation.**
8. **Active play should create both value and consumption pressure.**
9. **Punishment should be strongest in voluntarily accepted risky actions, not normal absence.**
10. **Failure should often generate new world content/recovery tasks.**
11. **Upgrades should primarily unlock new verbs, spaces and transformations.**
12. **Important progression should remain visibly embodied in the room.**
13. **Office Final Polish must preserve functional negative space for future systems.**
